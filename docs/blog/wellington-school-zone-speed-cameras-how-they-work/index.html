Wellington School Zone Speed Cameras: Fines, Hours, Where the Money Goes

By Ben SiegelPublished November 4, 20265 min read

Since September 2025, speed cameras at 11 Wellington school zones have been mailing $100 notices to drivers caught going 11 mph or more over the limit while school is in session. Village data shows speeding and school-zone crashes dropping sharply. Some drivers call the program a money grab, and a court ruling elsewhere in South Florida has raised questions about whether these cameras are constitutional. Here's how the program works, where the money goes, and what each side says.

Wellington's school zone cameras at a glance

Where
11 school zones where the village has authority, including Somerset Academy; Palm Beach Central High is excluded because of jurisdiction issues on Forest Hill Blvd.
When
On school days, from about a half hour before school starts until a half hour after it ends, not only when the yellow lights flash
The trigger
11 mph or more over the speed limit in effect at that moment
The fine
$100 notice of violation, mailed to the registered owner
Vendor
Jenoptik Smart Mobility Solutions, which installs the cameras at no upfront cost to the village
The law
A 2023 Florida law allows local governments to use speed cameras in school zones after passing an ordinance

How it works

The cameras went live with a 30-day warning period starting Aug. 11, 2025, and $100 fines began in September, the Town-Crier reported. During the warning period, more than 10,000 drivers got warnings in the mail.

The cameras enforce the posted limit all school day. When the yellow lights flash, the lower school-zone limit (commonly 20 mph) applies. Between drop-off and pick-up, the regular limit applies, and the cameras still issue notices to drivers going 11 mph or more over it. Enforcement hours vary by school. For example, they ran from 7 a.m. to 2:25 p.m. at Binks Forest Elementary and from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. at Emerald Cove Middle in the schedule shown to the village in 2025.

Drivers have 30 days to pay or contest a notice, typically through a hearing before a village magistrate. The village also said it was reviewing signage and visibility at school zones and could go "above and beyond minimum requirements" at some locations, WFLX reported.

Where the $100 goes

According to a breakdown the village gave WFLX:

  • $50 to the Village of Wellington
  • $23 to the Florida Department of Revenue
  • $12 to the school district for school security, student transportation or safer walking conditions
  • $10 to the camera vendor, Jenoptik
  • $5 to the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office

From mid-September 2025 to early April 2026, the cameras produced 18,757 notices of violation. Collections from September through March topped $1.05 million, with $588,105 going to the village, according to data presented to the Village Council in May.

The case for the cameras

  • Less speeding. Village data showed a 58% drop in speed violations from October 2025 to April 2026, the Town-Crier reported.
  • Fewer crashes. Only 15 crashes had been reported in Wellington's school zones through May 13, 2026, a 66.7% decrease compared with 2025.
  • Safety, not tickets. "The goal of this program was never to issue citations. It is to encourage drivers to slow down and create a safer environment around our schools," Public Safety Director Nicole Coates said. "Success of the program is not measured by violations issued; it is measured by safer driving behavior."
  • Cheaper than officers. Village Manager Jim Barnes told WFLX that using deputies instead would increase the law enforcement budget, and the cameras are a more efficient way to watch school zones. "School zones haven't changed, the times of the school zones haven't changed, the posted speeds haven't changed. We are just enforcing it," he said.
  • A real problem. A two-week study at two Wellington school zones before the program found 543 vehicles going at least 11 mph over the limit, including 11 going faster than 60 mph, the Town-Crier reported.

The case for caution

  • "Money grab" concerns. Some residents have called the program a money grab and asked for more transparency about where ticket money goes, WFLX reported. The village says the program is funded by paid violations and the money goes back into the safety program.
  • All-day enforcement surprises drivers. Because the cameras run all school day, drivers can get a notice when the lights aren't flashing. One driver told WFLX she was ticketed near Wellington Landings Middle when the lights weren't on; her ticket was later dismissed.
  • Accuracy questions. Barnes confirmed a speed discrepancy of less than 1 mph in some cameras and said those citations were not sent out.
  • Legal uncertainty. A Broward County circuit judge ruled this year that fining drivers with these cameras is unconstitutional because it puts the burden of proof on the vehicle owner, the Town-Crier reported. The case is on appeal. Palm Beach County paused its own plan for cameras in unincorporated school zones while it plays out. "I would hate to have a third party make investments throughout the county, and then be told we can't enforce," Commissioner Joel Flores said.

What happens next

Wellington plans to keep the program running. "Wellington's program will continue in accordance with applicable Florida law and any guidance provided through the legal process," Coates said. "If future court decisions or legislative changes require adjustments, the village and our technology partner are prepared to implement any necessary changes." Neighboring communities are also weighing cameras and plate readers; see our look at license plate readers and privacy rules. For other traffic changes near schools, see Wellington's new roundabouts.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a school zone speed camera ticket in Wellington, FL?

A notice of violation is $100, mailed to the registered owner. Of that, $50 goes to the Village of Wellington, $23 to the Florida Department of Revenue, $12 to the school district, $10 to the camera vendor and $5 to the Sheriff's Office, according to the village.

When are Wellington's school zone speed cameras on?

They operate on school days from about a half hour before school starts until a half hour after it ends, not only when the yellow lights flash. Drivers going 11 mph or more over the speed limit in effect at that moment can get a notice.

How many schools have speed cameras in Wellington?

Eleven school zones where the village has authority, including Somerset Academy. Palm Beach Central High is excluded because of jurisdiction issues on Forest Hill Blvd., the Town-Crier reported.

Can I contest a Wellington speed camera ticket?

Yes. Drivers have 30 days to pay or contest a notice, typically through a hearing before a village magistrate.
