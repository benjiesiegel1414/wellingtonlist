K-Park Becomes Village Landing: Did Wellington Make the Right Call?

By Wellington List staffPublished October 19, 20266 min read
Advertisement Sponsor WellingtonList.comBecome one of 10 local sponsors featured on every page of the site, for $49/month. Claim a spot

For more than 20 years, K-Park was the biggest piece of open land on State Road 7, and residents never stopped arguing about what it should become. In 2025, the Village Council finally decided: sell it for a walkable town center and a private school. Now construction is starting on Village Landing, and the debate has shifted to a new question. Did Wellington make the right call, especially after turning down a higher offer? Here's the full story and both sides.

K-Park at a glance

The land
About 70 acres on State Road 7 at Stribling Way, south of The Mall at Wellington Green
Bought
2003, from the Kahlert family, for $8.5 million
Sold
In two parts for about $47 million: a town center to Related Ross and a school site to the founders of Wingrove Academy
The road not taken
A $54 million offer for the whole property from a Ward family entity
What's coming
Village Landing (restaurants, shops, offices and a 180-room hotel) and Wingrove Academy, both targeting 2028

Two decades of "what if"

Wellington bought the land in 2003 from the Kahlert family, which is where the "K" comes from, for $8.5 million, according to the Palm Beach Post. The original idea was a park, but the village built its parks elsewhere. Over the years K-Park was floated for a college campus, senior living, a research center, a horse park and a botanical garden. None stuck. In the meantime, the land was leased to J. Alderman Farms for organic vegetables. At meetings in 2014 and 2015, many residents said they wanted it kept as open space.

The decision

In January 2025, the Village Council unanimously agreed to sell the property in two pieces, according to WFLX: one to Related Ross, the team behind West Palm Beach's CityPlace, for a mixed-use town center, and one to the founders of an international school group for a private school. The Palm Beach Post reported the total was about $47 million, and that the Village chose Related Ross over a $54 million offer for all of K-Park from the Ward family's W & W VIII LLC, citing in part Related's reputation as an experienced, national builder and counting the school as a plus.

In 2026, Related Ross paid $28.3 million for its 32 acres at 10400 Stribling Way, Commercial Observer reported, and the roughly 44-acre western portion is set aside for Wingrove Academy. Under the plan revised in May 2026, Village Landing will have about 220,000 square feet of shops, 100,000 square feet of restaurants, 90,000 square feet of offices, a 180-room hotel and a central lake with a boardwalk. An earlier version included about 300 apartments, which were dropped.

The case that Wellington got it right

  • A school residents asked for. "We are a private school desert," Mayor Michael Napoleone said when the sale was approved, adding that "having a private school in Wellington is a great value for our community."
  • A real downtown. Supporters say Wellington has never had a walkable center for dining and gathering. A local pastor told WFLX it would give families places to eat and go out "literally" across the street.
  • The higher bid isn't the whole story. The Village says it weighed the developer's track record and the school, not just the price. Supporters argue a proven builder and a long-term tax base may be worth more than the $7 million difference.
  • A big return either way. The Village paid $8.5 million in 2003 and sold for about $47 million.

The case that it didn't

  • Residents wanted green space. A group called Wellington 67 pushed to keep the land green, proposing a large botanical garden, and pointed to resident surveys from 2015 that favored open space. "People are so connected to nature, they don't want it all to be a concrete jungle," a member told WFLX.
  • Money left on the table. Critics point out the Village accepted about $7 million less than the highest offer for the same land.
  • Traffic. Traffic was one of the biggest concerns at council meetings. The approvals require new roundabouts, extra lanes around Stribling Way and added turn lanes at Forest Hill Boulevard and State Road 7, but skeptics worry it won't be enough once the school, hotel and shops are all open.
  • Taller buildings. In September 2026 the Council approved a conditional use allowing four buildings between 37 and 47.5 feet, above the village's usual 35-foot threshold, which some residents see as a step away from Wellington's low-rise look.

What happens next

Construction on Village Landing is expected to begin this fall, with an opening targeted for 2028, the same year Wingrove Academy hopes to welcome its first students. The real verdict on K-Park will come then, when residents can see whether it feels like the downtown Wellington was promised or just more traffic on State Road 7.

What do you think?

Was selling K-Park for a town center and school the right move, or should Wellington have kept it green or taken the higher offer? For more on the corridor, see why Lyons Road still dead-ends and the debate over apartments at the old Nordstrom.

Frequently asked questions

What is K-Park in Wellington?

K-Park is about 70 acres on State Road 7 at Stribling Way that the Village of Wellington bought in 2003 for $8.5 million. It is becoming Village Landing and Wingrove Academy.

Who bought K-Park?

Related Ross bought about 32 acres for $28.3 million for Village Landing, and the western portion is set aside for Wingrove Academy. The total deal was about $47 million.

Did Wellington turn down a higher offer for K-Park?

Yes. The Palm Beach Post reported a $54 million offer for all of K-Park from the Ward family's W & W VIII LLC. The Village chose Related Ross, citing in part its experience, and counted the school as a plus.

When will Village Landing open?

Construction is expected to begin in fall 2026, with an opening targeted for 2028.
