    Wellington Community Foundation: Helping Seniors, Children and Veterans

    By Ben SiegelPublished November 28, 20266 min read

    Wellington has a reputation as an affluent village, but plenty of neighbors here still need a hand: seniors trying to stay in their homes, veterans who can't afford repairs, and kids who need school uniforms or a summer camp spot. The Wellington Community Foundation is the local nonprofit that focuses on exactly those three groups. It just received a $20,000 grant from the Quantum Foundation. Here's what the foundation does, its programs and scholarships, and how to help or get help.

    The $20,000 Quantum grant

    The foundation was among the nonprofits honored at the sixth annual Quantum in the Community Breakfast on Nov. 17, 2026, where it received a $20,000 grant to use in Wellington, the Town-Crier reported. The Quantum Foundation is a Palm Beach County health-focused funder. The grant supports the foundation's work with the community's most vulnerable seniors, children and veterans.

    "The Wellington Community Foundation extends its sincere appreciation to Quantum Foundation for the $20,000 award and for its continued commitment to strengthening local communities," foundation Chair Jim Sackett said. Executive Director Dawn Rivera called it "a wonderful morning celebrating those who make a difference in our community."

    How the foundation started

    According to its website, the Wellington Community Foundation was created in 2009 as a nonprofit community service arm of the Village of Wellington, led by the Village Council. New rules later barred elected officials from raising money through that kind of entity, so the foundation was handed to a community-run board. Under former Wellington Mayor Tom Wenham, the revitalized foundation held its first fundraiser in June 2016 to buy backpacks for students in need.

    The foundation still works closely with the village. It signed a memorandum of understanding with the Village Council to coordinate on community projects, with the village's Community Services Department helping identify residents who need help.

    The Arle & Ken Adams Scholarship

    The foundation's first scholarship honors former Palm Beach County Commissioner Ken Adams, an early board member who died in 2020, and his late wife, Arle. For the 2026 round:

    • Who can apply: high school seniors who live in Wellington or attend school in Wellington.
    • Award: a one-time $1,500 educational scholarship.
    • What to send: two recommendation letters (ideally from core-subject teachers), a transcript, proof of community service hours, a signed application and short essay answers.
    • How it's scored: academics 25%, community service 25%, leadership 20%, extracurriculars or work 10%, overcoming adversity 10% and the overall application 10%.
    • Timeline: the 2026 round was open Jan. 1 through April 24 and is now closed, with awards presented in May. Watch the foundation's site for the next round, likely in early 2027.

    Events

    The foundation's main fundraiser is its annual Red, White & Blue Jeans: A Salute to Our Heroes evening. The 11th annual event was held Nov. 13, 2026, at Wellington National Golf Club. Sponsorships and tickets help fund the programs above.

    How to help

    • Donate: through the donate links on wellingtoncommunityfoundation.org. As a 501(c)(3), gifts are generally tax-deductible; check with your tax advisor.
    • Volunteer: the foundation's "Volunteer Today" program has an online application. Questions go to wellingtoncommunityfoundation@gmail.com
    • Sponsor an event: local businesses can sponsor the Red, White & Blue Jeans event.
    • Give this season: see our holiday toy drive and volunteer guide for more ways to give back.

    How to get help

    If you're a Wellington senior, veteran or family in need, start with the village's Community Services Department at (561) 791-4000, which works with the foundation to identify residents who qualify, or call the foundation at (561) 603-9931. Older adults can also see our guide to senior programs and classes in Wellington.

    Why local giving matters now

    Local nonprofits are bracing for leaner years. Amendment 3 is expected to cut property tax funding that flows to agencies like the Children's Services Council, and nonprofit leaders say private donations will have to fill more of the gap. Supporters of the amendment say lower taxes leave more money in residents' pockets, some of which can go to local charities. Either way, small, local groups like the Wellington Community Foundation depend on neighbors and local businesses.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the Wellington Community Foundation?

    A 501(c)(3) nonprofit that helps Wellington's seniors, children and veterans in need. It began in 2009 as the village's community service arm and is now run by a community board.

    What grant did the Wellington Community Foundation receive in 2026?

    A $20,000 Quantum in the Community grant, presented at the Quantum Foundation's breakfast on Nov. 17, 2026, the Town-Crier reported.

    How do I apply for the Arle & Ken Adams Scholarship?

    High school seniors who live or attend school in Wellington can apply for the one-time $1,500 award. The 2026 round ran Jan. 1 to April 24; watch the foundation's website for the next round.

    How can I get help from the Wellington Community Foundation?

    Call the foundation at (561) 603-9931 or the village's Community Services Department at (561) 791-4000, which helps identify residents who qualify.
