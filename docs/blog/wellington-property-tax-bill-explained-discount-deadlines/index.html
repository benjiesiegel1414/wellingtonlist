Your Wellington Property Tax Bill Explained: 4% Discount, Deadlines and What Each Line Pays For

By Ben SiegelPublished November 20, 20266 min read

Your 2026 property tax bill arrived in early November, and if you live in Wellington, Florida, it has a lot of lines on it: the village, the county, the school district, fire rescue, Acme Improvement District, solid waste and more. Here's how to read the bill, how the village's share is calculated, and how to save money by paying early. Paying by Nov. 30 gets you a 4% discount, and the discount shrinks every month after that.

Pay early and save: the discount calendar

Florida rewards early payment. The Palm Beach County Tax Collector mails more than 600,000 property tax bills by Nov. 1, and they're payable Nov. 1 through March 31:

December3%Pay $5,820, save $180
January2%Pay $5,880, save $120

The examples are our math. If a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, Florida law moves it to the next business day. Once taxes are delinquent, the Tax Collector says they can't be paid online and a postmark isn't proof of payment.

Have a mortgage escrow? Your lender usually pays the bill for you, typically in November to get the 4% discount. Your bill may be marked as sent to the lender. Check with your mortgage company before paying it yourself so you don't pay twice.

The installment plan

If one big payment is hard, Palm Beach County offers an Installment Payment Plan under Florida law (Section 197.222, Florida Statutes). You pay in four quarterly installments, with smaller discounts built in on the early ones. You have to sign up ahead of time; under state law, applications are due by April 30 for the following tax year. If you're already enrolled, the Tax Collector lists Dec. 31 as the due date for the third installment. Details and the application are on the Tax Collector's website.

How to read your bill

A Wellington bill has two main parts.

1. Ad valorem taxes (based on value)

These are the taxes calculated from your home's value. Each taxing authority sets its own rate, called a millage rate. Typical lines on a Wellington bill include:

  • Village of Wellington: 2.47 mills, which covers village operations including sheriff's patrol. The council kept the rate the same for fiscal 2027; see our story on why Wellington didn't lower its tax rate.
  • Palm Beach County Fire Rescue: a separate rate, which the village's tax page lists at 3.46 mills, because the county provides fire rescue in Wellington.
  • Palm Beach County, the School District of Palm Beach County, the South Florida Water Management District, the Health Care District and the Children's Services Council, among others.

2. Non-ad valorem assessments (flat fees)

These aren't based on value. On a Wellington bill they commonly include:

  • Wellington Solid Waste: pays for collecting and transporting your trash and recycling (see our trash and recycling guide).
  • Solid Waste Authority of Palm Beach County: pays for disposal, a separate charge.
  • Acme Improvement District: a special district created by the Florida Legislature in 1953 for drainage and water management in western Palm Beach County. It's charged in units based on land size: 1 unit up to 1.49 acres, 2 units for 1.50 to 2.49 acres, 3 units for 2.50 to 3.49 acres, and 4 units for 3.50 to 4.49 acres.

Some neighborhoods, such as Saddle Trail, have their own extra assessments. If you live in a community with a separate drainage or water control district, that will show up too.

How the village's share is calculated

The formula, according to the village: taxable value ÷ 1,000 × millage rate = tax.

The bill shows three values:

  • Market value: what the property would likely sell for.
  • Assessed value: market value after any cap. Homesteaded homes are protected by Florida's Save Our Homes cap; non-homestead properties have a 10% cap on yearly increases.
  • Taxable value: assessed value minus exemptions such as homestead, senior or veteran exemptions.

Example from the village: a home with a $450,000 assessed value and $50,000 in exemptions has a $400,000 taxable value. At 2.47 mills, the Wellington portion is $400,000 ÷ 1,000 × 2.47 = $988. That's only the village's slice; the school district, county and fire rescue usually make up much more of the total.

Homestead basics

If the home is your permanent residence and you owned it on Jan. 1, Florida's homestead exemption takes $25,000 off the first $50,000 of assessed value for all taxes, plus up to another $25,000 on value between $50,000 and $75,000 for non-school taxes. Missed it? See our guide on how to file for homestead in Palm Beach County. The filing deadline for next year's exemption is March 1.

Amendment 3: Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on Nov. 3, 2026, expanding homestead exemptions. It doesn't change the 2026 bills you just got. Read our explainer on what Amendment 3 means for Palm Beach County homeowners.

Think your value is wrong?

The Tax Collector only collects the bill. Values and exemptions are set by the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser. The time to challenge your value was after the TRIM notice in August, through the Value Adjustment Board. If you believe something is wrong, contact the Property Appraiser's office. Don't wait to pay, though: an appeal doesn't stop the March 31 deadline.

How to pay

  • Online through the Tax Collector's website (before taxes become delinquent).
  • In person at a Tax Collector service center. Check the Tax Collector's site for the nearest location and hours (general office hours are Monday to Friday, 8:15 a.m. to 5 p.m.). Offices are closed Dec. 24 and 25.
  • By mail, using the coupon from your bill.

Check the Tax Collector's Payment Options page for any card or processing fees before you pay.

Two views on Wellington's tax rate

Some residents argue that keeping the rate flat while values rise is effectively a tax increase, and a council member pushed for a cut this year. Supporters of holding the rate point to the village's lower budget and the uncertainty Amendment 3 created for future revenue. Either way, the village rate is a small share of the total bill compared with the school district and county.

Frequently asked questions

When are Palm Beach County property taxes due?

Bills are mailed by Nov. 1 and are payable Nov. 1 through March 31. Taxes become delinquent on April 1 and are subject to penalties and interest.

What is the discount for paying property taxes early in Palm Beach County?

4% in November, 3% in December, 2% in January and 1% in February. There's no discount in March.

What is Wellington's property tax rate?

Wellington's rate is 2.47 mills, or $2.47 per $1,000 of taxable value, unchanged for fiscal 2027. A home with $400,000 in taxable value pays about $988 to the village.

What is the Acme Improvement District charge on my Wellington tax bill?

Acme is a special district created in 1953 for drainage and water management. It charges a flat, non-ad valorem assessment in units based on land size, starting at 1 unit for parcels up to 1.49 acres.

Who do I call about my property tax bill?

Call the Palm Beach County Tax Collector at 561-355-2264 for payments. Questions about your home's value or exemptions go to the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser.
