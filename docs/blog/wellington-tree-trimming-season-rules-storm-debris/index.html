    Wellington Tree Trimming Season Starts Dec. 1: Rules, Permits and Storm Debris Pickup

    By Ben SiegelPublished November 21, 20267 min read

    Hurricane season ends Nov. 30, and that makes Dec. 1 the start of tree trimming season in Wellington, Florida. The village asks residents to do all major tree cutting and removal between Dec. 1 and April 30, and not at all from June through November. Here's the calendar, when you need a permit, how much you're allowed to cut, the pruning mistakes that can get you fined, how to put branches out for pickup, and how storm debris collection works if a hurricane does hit.

    Rules and tips are from the Village of Wellington's Hurricane Debris Management, Tree Care Tips, Vegetation Collection and Tree Removal pages and its Emergency Management and Landscape Regulations FAQs, checked Nov. 21, 2026. HOA rules may add their own requirements.

    The Wellington tree calendar

    WhenWhat the village asks
    Dec. 1 to April 30Do all major cutting and tree removal. Cut trees back from buildings and power lines and thin canopies so wind can pass through.
    MayDon't wait until now. The village says if everyone cut in May, haulers couldn't collect it all before a June storm.
    June 1 to Nov. 30No major cutting. Routine yard maintenance only.
    Once a storm is namedDon't cut trees, do major yard work, start construction projects or clean out the garage.

    Why the rule? Big piles of branches at the curb become flying debris in a hurricane. The village says that when residents cut heavily as a storm approaches, there isn't enough manpower, equipment or time to collect it, and haulers also have to secure their own facilities. Trucks can't be on the road once sustained winds top 35 mph.

    Do you need a permit?

    • Trimming: no permit to trim up to 25% of a tree or palm, according to the village's landscape FAQ. Over-trimming isn't allowed and can lead to required removal and replacement, plus possible fines.
    • Removing a tree or palm: you need a Tree/Vegetation Removal permit from Planning and Zoning. That includes fruit trees, even though they don't count toward your required landscaping.
    • Neighbor's branches: you may trim what hangs over your property line. The village suggests talking to your neighbor first, especially if heavy trimming could harm the tree.
    • Trees on private property are the owner's job. The village doesn't remove private trees, and after a storm, cutting up a fallen tree is the homeowner's or HOA's responsibility.

    Every Wellington property is also required to keep a minimum amount of landscaping, including shade trees, so removing a tree may mean planting a replacement. See our hedge, fence and landscaping rules guide.

    How to trim the right way

    The village's Tree Care Tips page follows standard arborist practice. The key numbers:

    • Mature trees: remove no more than 20% to 25% of the foliage at one time.
    • Young trees: no more than one-third at a time.
    • One trunk is best. Trees with a single dominant trunk are stronger; trees with multiple trunks are more likely to split in a storm.
    • Thin, don't top. Thinning cuts that take a branch back to the trunk or parent limb are preferred. They reduce weight and let wind through while keeping the tree's shape.
    • Cut outside the branch collar, never flush with the trunk, and don't leave stubs.
    • Skip the pruning paint. The village says it doesn't prevent decay and can slow healing.

    Three mistakes to avoid

    MistakeWhat it isWhy it's a problem
    Topping ("hat racking")Cutting off the top of the canopy and leaving stubsCauses decay, attracts wood-boring insects and makes trees more likely to fail in storms
    Overlifting ("lion-tailing")Stripping too many lower and interior branchesMakes the tree top-heavy and more prone to branch damage
    Removing green palm frondsThe "hurricane cut" that leaves only a few frondsRobs the palm of nutrients, weakens the trunk and can cause it to snap in high winds

    For palms, the village says it's fine to remove brown and yellow fronds hanging below an imaginary horizontal line, but leave the green ones.

    Hiring a tree service

    The village recommends an ISA Certified Arborist or a member of a professional arborist association, with a business license and insurance for property damage and personal liability. Ask for proof of both. Browse local tree services and landscapers in our directory.

    Putting branches at the curb

    If you do the work yourself, Wellington's weekly yard waste pickup will take it, within limits:

    • No piece longer than 6 feet or heavier than 50 pounds, and no limbs over 10 inches in diameter.
    • Bag or containerize leaves, small trimmings and fronds.
    • Keep piles 3 feet from other trash, and not under power lines or on vacant lots or canal banks.
    • Extra yard waste can go out no more than 24 hours before your collection day.
    • Not collected: tree removals, trunks, stumps and root balls.

    If you hire a landscaper or tree company, they must haul away their own debris. Piles that break the rules get tagged; you can fix the pile or pay the hauler $9 per cubic yard to take it. Full details are in our trash, recycling and yard waste guide.

    If a hurricane hits: how storm debris pickup works

    Storm cleanup runs on different rules from weekly pickup. From the village's Emergency Management FAQ:

    • Who collects it: Wellington hires private contractors, and FEMA representatives interpret and enforce the collection rules.
    • Garbage first. Regular garbage is the first thing collected after a storm. Put it out on your normal day unless told otherwise.
    • Timing: debris collection starts almost immediately, but it may be 3 to 6 weeks before your first pickup. The whole cleanup generally takes 45 to 120 days.
    • Separate your piles: vegetation, construction debris, fencing and household garbage each go in their own pile at the curb, away from power lines, water meters, mailboxes and hydrants, and never in the road.
    • First pass is clean vegetation only. Mixed piles wait for the second or third pass. Contractors made at least two passes after the 2004 and 2005 storms.
    • Bring it to the curb. Under FEMA guidelines, contractors can't come into your yard.
    • Stumps are collected by a separate contractor, usually mid to late in the cleanup.
    • Rake up after. Leaves, twigs and pine needles left behind are the homeowner's job to bag for the next pass.
    • Expect some lawn damage. Heavy equipment can rut swales and lawns; the village says residents should be prepared to add fill and sod.
    • Roof tiles and shingles: double-bag small amounts. For large amounts, rent a roll-off or have your roofer haul them.

    What's not covered: businesses must arrange their own storm debris removal. Condo and apartment communities with dumpster service must hire a company to move debris to the nearest public road. And if a community hires a private contractor instead of waiting, the village says there's no reimbursement.

    Two views on the seasonal rule

    Some homeowners find the timing inconvenient, since trees grow fastest in the rainy summer months and overgrown limbs seem most worrying right when storms threaten. The village's position is that winter trimming is exactly what keeps those limbs from becoming a problem, and that summer piles put neighbors at risk. Routine maintenance is still allowed year-round; it's the major cutting and removals the village wants done by April 30.

    Frequently asked questions

    When should I trim trees in Wellington, FL?

    The village asks residents to do all major tree cutting and removal between Dec. 1 and April 30, and no major cutting from June 1 through Nov. 30, which is hurricane season.

    Do I need a permit to trim or remove a tree in Wellington?

    You can trim up to 25% of a tree or palm without a permit. Removing a tree or palm, including a fruit tree, requires a Tree/Vegetation Removal permit from the village.

    Will Wellington pick up tree branches at the curb?

    Yes, on your weekly yard waste day if pieces are under 6 feet long and 50 pounds and limbs are under 10 inches in diameter. Tree removals, trunks and stumps aren't collected, and contractors must haul away their own debris.

    How long does storm debris pickup take in Wellington?

    Collection starts almost immediately after a storm, but the village says it may be 3 to 6 weeks before your first pickup, and full cleanup generally takes 45 to 120 days.

    Is it OK to cut green palm fronds before a hurricane?

    No. The village says removing healthy green fronds weakens the palm and can cause the trunk to snap in high winds. Only remove brown and yellow fronds hanging below horizontal.
