Locksmith license in Florida?
None. Florida has no state locksmith license, so check reviews, ID and a real business name.

First: a child or pet locked in a car

A closed car in Florida heats up fast. If a child, a vulnerable adult or a pet is locked inside, call 911 right away; police and fire-rescue can get in faster than any locksmith. Florida law (Section 768.139) protects a person who breaks into a car to rescue a vulnerable person or a household pet from liability for the damage, as long as the car is locked, they believe in good faith the person or animal is in imminent danger, they call 911 or law enforcement before or immediately after going in, they use no more force than needed, and they stay until first responders arrive.

Pop-A-Lock also runs a free 24/7 "PAL Saves Kids" program that opens cars with children locked inside at no charge. Still call 911 first.

Locked out of your car

  • Check your roadside assistance first. Many auto insurance policies, AAA memberships, new-car warranties and credit cards include lockout service at no extra cost.
  • Lost all your keys? Modern cars use transponder chips or smart fobs that must be programmed to the car. Pop-A-Lock, A Local Locksmith and South Shore Locksmiths list car key replacement; a dealer can also do it, though it may mean a tow.
  • Need a spare? The Minute Key kiosk inside the Lowe's at 103 S State Road 7 in Royal Palm Beach copies standard keys and many key fobs and car keys.

Just moved to Wellington? Rekey your locks

Wellington sees a steady stream of new residents and seasonal renters, and you never know how many copies of the old keys are out there. Rekeying changes the lock's internal pins so old keys stop working, and it's usually cheaper than replacing the whole lock. Ask the locksmith to key every exterior door to one key, and don't forget the garage entry door and any side gates. If you'd rather do it yourself, rekeyable locks and smart locks are sold at the Home Depot on State Road 7.

How to spot a locksmith scam

Locksmith searches are full of fake listings. When we researched this guide, most results for "Wellington locksmith" were call centers using made-up names, borrowed addresses and toll-free or out-of-area numbers that send your call to whoever is available. Palm Beach County Consumer Affairs points out that Florida has no professional licensing for locksmiths, so anyone can call themselves one. Tips from the county and the Federal Trade Commission:

  • Pick a locksmith before you need one. Save a vetted number in your phone now.
  • Ask for the legal business name when you call. Be wary if they answer with a generic "locksmith services."
  • Get the full price up front, including the service call, labor, parts and any late-night or mileage fee. Ultra-low ads like "$19 service call" are a common bait.
  • Don't let work start if the price jumps when they arrive, and never sign a blank authorization.
  • Be skeptical of "we have to drill it." A skilled locksmith can open most residential locks without drilling.
  • Check ID and the vehicle. The name on their ID, van, invoice and business card should match.
  • Expect to be asked for proof that you live there or own the car. A real locksmith will ask.
  • Get an itemized invoice with the business name and address.

ALOA certification, from the industry's main trade association, is a sign a locksmith has passed competency testing. Every business in Palm Beach County also needs a local business tax receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a 24-hour locksmith in Wellington, FL?

Yes. Pop-A-Lock of Palm Beach (561) 659-6736, A Local Locksmith (561) 351-8898 and South Shore Locksmiths (561) 531-2619 advertise 24/7 service for home and car lockouts in Wellington.

What should I do if a child or pet is locked in a car?

Call 911 immediately rather than waiting for a locksmith. Florida Statute 768.139 protects people who break into a locked car to rescue a vulnerable person or household pet in imminent danger, as long as they call 911 before or right after and stay until first responders arrive.

Do locksmiths need a license in Florida?

No. Palm Beach County Consumer Affairs notes there is no professional licensing for locksmiths in Florida, so ask for the legal business name, ID and a written price, and look for ALOA certification.

Should I rekey or replace my locks after moving?

Rekeying is usually the cheaper option. It changes the pins inside your existing locks so old keys no longer work. Replace the locks if they are worn, damaged or you want a smart lock or higher-security hardware.

Where can I get keys copied in Wellington?

The Home Depot at 220 S State Road 7 offers key cutting and is open until 11 p.m. Monday through Saturday. The Minute Key kiosk at the Lowe's at 103 S State Road 7 in Royal Palm Beach copies keys, fobs and some car keys.
