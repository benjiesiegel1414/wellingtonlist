Every Playground in Wellington, FL: All 21 Compared (2026 Guide)

By Ben SiegelPublished November 13, 20267 min read

Wellington has 21 public playgrounds run by the village, from the ocean-themed Tiger Shark Cove to the barrier-free Scott's Place and 17 small neighborhood parks most families never hear about. We went through every park in the Village of Wellington's facilities directory and pulled together the address, hours and amenities for each one, so you can see which has restrooms, which has a pavilion and which is closest to you, all on one page.

1. Tiger Shark Cove: best all-around playground

Tiger Shark Cove on Greenbriar Boulevard is Wellington's showpiece playground, with an ocean theme kids remember: a large tiger shark play sculpture, a sunken pirate ship play area, a submarine with portholes, a sand castle, a crab shack, obstacle courses and swing sets. The village says it has shaded areas and soft, rubberized flooring, plus restrooms and water stations. The park also includes Leather's Playground, which was designed by village residents and built by hundreds of volunteers.

  • Hours: Sunrise to sunset (8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Nov. 25-27 for Thanksgiving)
  • Pavilions: Two small pavilions with 2 picnic tables each, and one large pavilion with 10 tables connected to the restrooms. None has grills or electricity.
  • Also on site: 4 softball fields, a concession stand, an outdoor basketball court and a multi-purpose field.

Tip: Pavilions are free on weekdays, first come, first served. On Saturdays and Sundays you need a rental (see pavilion prices below).

2. Scott's Place: best for children of all abilities

Scott's Place Barrier-Free Playground at Town Center is designed to meet the needs of all children. It was made possible by a donation from Wellington residents Del and Barbara Williamson in honor of their son Scott, who passed away at age six, and more than 300 volunteers assembled the equipment during a community build weekend. It opened March 1, 2010. It has a pavilion, picnic areas and restrooms, and it sits next to the Wellington Amphitheater, so it's easy to pair with a free concert or movie night.

  • Hours: Monday through Saturday 7:30 a.m. to 9 p.m.; Sunday 7:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. That makes it the best after-dinner playground in Wellington.
  • Heads up: Scott's Place is closed for maintenance Monday, Nov. 16 through Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, and reopens when the work is done, weather permitting.

3. Village Park: best for a full day out

Village Park on Pierson Road is home to the village's Parks and Recreation offices and has one playground, flanked by two of the park's three rental pavilions. What makes it a full-day trip is everything around the playground: a 2.5-mile asphalt walking trail with exercise stations, a new aquatics center next door, a skate park, a covered roller hockey rink and Peaceful Waters Sanctuary's boardwalks.

  • Hours: Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.; Saturday and Sunday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
  • Pavilions by the playground: Pavilion 2 (left of the playground) has 2 grills and 10 picnic tables; Pavilion 3 (right of the playground) is large, with 1 big grill and 12 picnic tables. Neither has electricity.
  • Skate park rules: helmets are required, and skaters under 11 must be with someone 18 or older.

4. Greenbriar Park: best if the dog is coming

Greenbriar Park has a swing set and play structure, restrooms and a pavilion, and it's right next to the Wellington Dog Park, so one parent can watch the kids while the other takes the dog into the fenced, off-leash area. The park also has six outdoor pickleball courts, 4 sand volleyball courts with shade structures and 2 open grass fields. It's open sunrise to sunset.

5. The 17 neighborhood playgrounds

The rest of Wellington's playgrounds are small neighborhood parks tucked into communities like Greenview Shores, South Shore and the Landings. They're quiet, rarely crowded and usually a short walk or bike ride from home. Most have a play structure and swings, and many have a covered pavilion, which is free to use on a first come, first served basis. Eight also have a basketball court, which makes them a good fit for families with a toddler and an older kid.

The village's listings for the neighborhood parks don't include restrooms, so plan on short visits. A few worth knowing:

  • For a playground plus basketball: Azure, Farmington, Foresteria, Margate and Primrose parks have a play structure, swings, a pavilion and a basketball court.
  • Margate Park in Greenview Shores got a new playground structure in 2022, according to the village.
  • Block Island Park in South Shore has a playground, swings, a pavilion and open grass.

Pavilion rental prices

Pavilion rentals are only needed on Saturdays and Sundays at Tiger Shark Cove and Village Park. On weekdays, and at every neighborhood park, pavilions are free, first come, first served. Weekend rates include sales tax:

PavilionHalf dayFull day
Small (Village Park 1 and 2, Tiger Shark Cove 1 and 2)$53$106
Large (Village Park 3, Tiger Shark Cove 3)$80$160

A half day is 8 a.m. to noon or 1 to 5 p.m.; a full day is 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. You can book up to 60 days ahead online through the village's CivicRec catalog or at the Village Park gym front desk. For dates within 7 days, you have to book in person at Village Park. The village's rental page lists no bounce houses or inflatables for the Tiger Shark Cove pavilions. Bonus: the splash-and-play option

The Wellington Aquatics Center at 3410 120th Ave. S isn't free, but its recreation side has a zero-depth entry pool with a play structure, kept at 83 to 85 degrees, so it works year round. It's open Monday through Saturday 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. and Sunday noon to 7 p.m. from Labor Day to Memorial Day. Children under 13 must be with an adult. Prices and passes are in our Aquatics Center guide.

Playgrounds just outside Wellington

  • Okeeheelee Park, 7715 Forest Hill Blvd., has separate play structures for ages 2 to 5 and 5 to 12, according to Palm Beach County Parks, plus a nature center, fishing pier and pump track.
  • Crestwood Park North, 900 Crestwood Blvd. N in Royal Palm Beach, opened in 2024 with a playground, picnic pavilion, fishing boardwalk and courts, the Town-Crier reported.

Tips for Wellington playground days

  • Go early or late. Slides and play equipment get hot in the South Florida sun. Mornings and the hour before sunset are the most comfortable, and Scott's Place stays open until 9 p.m. most nights.
  • Pick the right park for little ones. If you need restrooms, stick to Tiger Shark Cove, Scott's Place, Village Park or Greenbriar Park.
  • Check closures. The village posts maintenance closures and weather status on its website.
  • Plan the rest of the day with our guides to things to do with kids in Wellington, the best parks in Wellington and kid-friendly restaurants.

Frequently asked questions

How many playgrounds are in Wellington, FL?

The Village of Wellington's facilities directory lists 21 parks with playgrounds: four larger parks (Tiger Shark Cove, Scott's Place, Village Park and Greenbriar Park) and 17 neighborhood parks.

What is the best playground in Wellington?

Tiger Shark Cove at 13800 Greenbriar Blvd. is the village's largest themed playground, with a pirate ship, submarine, sand castle, shark sculpture, shade, rubberized flooring and restrooms.

Which Wellington playgrounds have restrooms?

Tiger Shark Cove, Scott's Place, Village Park and Greenbriar Park list restrooms. The village's listings for the 17 neighborhood parks don't include restrooms.

How much does it cost to rent a pavilion in Wellington?

Weekend rentals at Tiger Shark Cove and Village Park are $53 for a half day or $106 for a full day for small pavilions, and $80 or $160 for large pavilions, including tax. Weekday use is free, first come, first served.

What are Scott's Place playground hours?

Scott's Place is open Monday through Saturday 7:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. and Sunday 7:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. It is closed for maintenance Nov. 16-20, 2026.
