Wellington's School Zone Speed Cameras, One Year In: Safer Streets or a Money Maker?

By Wellington List staffPublished October 25, 20264 min read

A year after Wellington switched on speed cameras in 11 school zones, village data shows a 58 percent drop in speeding violations and a two-thirds drop in school zone crashes. The program has also generated more than $1 million in fines. Now Westlake is adding cameras on Seminole Pratt Whitney Road, while Palm Beach County has put its own plan on hold because of a court challenge. Here's what Wellington's numbers show and why the debate isn't settled.

Wellington's school zone cameras at a glance

Started
August 2025, with a one-month warning period
Where
11 school zones in Wellington, run with vendor Jenoptik Smart Mobility Solutions
Fine
$100 for going 11 mph or more over the limit while school is in session
Results
58 percent fewer speed violations from October 2025 to April 2026; school zone crashes down 66.7 percent
Fines
More than $1.05 million collected from September 2025 to March 2026, with $588,105 going to Wellington

What the first year showed

Village staff presented the data to the council at a visioning workshop on May 13, 2026, the Town-Crier reported:

  • More than 10,000 warnings went out during the one-month warning period.
  • 18,757 notices of violation were issued from Sept. 15 to April 9.
  • Speed violations fell 58 percent from October 2025 to April 2026.
  • Only 15 crashes were reported in Wellington's school zones through May 13, a 66.7 percent decrease compared with 2025.

The case for the cameras

  • Drivers are slowing down. "These cameras have made people slow down in certain areas," Mayor Michael Napoleone said at the workshop.
  • Safety, not tickets. "The goal of this program was never to issue citations. It is to encourage drivers to slow down and create a safer environment around our schools," Public Safety Director Nicole Coates said. "Success of the program is not measured by violations issued; it is measured by safer driving behavior."
  • Who benefits. Coates said the results help "students walking or biking to school, parents during drop-off and pick-up, school staff and the entire community."
  • Children come first. On the county commission, Commissioner Gregg Weiss argued for moving ahead while the courts sort it out: "We could have everything ready to go and all buttoned up to be able to protect these kids."

The case for caution

  • A revenue source. The Town-Crier noted that, while not intentional, the program has become a lucrative revenue source for the village. County Mayor Sara Baxter, who voted against a county program, put it more bluntly: "I believe it is a money grab, and it is a financial way to bring money."
  • The legal cloud. A Broward County court ruled this spring that camera enforcement was unconstitutional because it put the burden of proof on vehicle owners, the Town-Crier reported. A Broward traffic judge also began dismissing pending school zone camera cases, CBS12 reported. Tallahassee Reports said in September that the Fourth District Court of Appeal is reviewing the related Broward ruling.
  • Other governments are holding off. Palm Beach County commissioners voted 6-1 in May against moving forward with cameras at 40 schools in unincorporated areas, including in The Acreage, until there is a court decision or a legislative fix. "I feel like we are putting an ordinance in place when we don't know who's going to provide the service, how this is going to be executed and whether the courts are essentially going to allow it," Commissioner Joel Flores said.

What happens next

Wellington plans to keep its program running. "Wellington's program will continue in accordance with applicable Florida law and any guidance provided through the legal process," Coates said. "If future court decisions or legislative changes require adjustments, the village and our technology partner are prepared to implement any necessary changes." Meanwhile, neighboring Westlake just approved its own cameras near Seminole Ridge High School. Read our breakdown: speed cameras and plate readers on Seminole Pratt.

What do you think?

Have Wellington's school zone cameras made you drive differently? Are they a proven safety tool worth keeping, or a revenue stream that should wait for the courts? For another debate about safety on Wellington's streets, see the village's e-bike rules.

Frequently asked questions

Does Wellington have school zone speed cameras?

Yes. Wellington launched cameras in 11 school zones in August 2025 with vendor Jenoptik Smart Mobility Solutions. Drivers going 11 mph or more over the limit while school is in session can be fined $100.

Have Wellington's school zone cameras reduced speeding?

According to village data presented in May 2026, speed violations fell 58 percent from October 2025 to April 2026, and school zone crashes were down 66.7 percent compared with 2025.

How much money have Wellington's speed cameras brought in?

From September 2025 to March 2026, fine collections totaled more than $1.05 million, with Wellington receiving $588,105, the Town-Crier reported.

Are school zone speed cameras legal in Florida?

A 2023 state law allows them, but a 2026 Broward County court ruling questioned camera enforcement and is under appeal. Palm Beach County paused its own program while the issue is resolved; Wellington's program continues.
