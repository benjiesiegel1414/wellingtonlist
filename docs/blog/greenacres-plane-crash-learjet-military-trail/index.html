Greenacres Plane Crash: What We Know About the Learjet Crash on Military Trail

By Wellington List staffPublished November 2, 20264 min read

Last updated: Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, late morning. This is a developing story. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) was expected to brief reporters at 1 p.m. Monday in West Palm Beach, and we'll update this page with verified information as it's released.

A Learjet 60 carrying nine people crashed near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in the Greenacres area of Palm Beach County on Sunday afternoon, Nov. 1, 2026, killing everyone on board. The business jet came down in a residential area near a busy roadway, east of Wellington, leaving a crater in the road and forcing about 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community to evacuate. Officials said no one on the ground was reported hurt. Here is what has been confirmed so far, and what it means for getting around the area.

What we know

When
Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026, at about 12:50 p.m., according to U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy
Where
Near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in the Greenacres area, about 7 miles southwest of West Palm Beach
Aircraft
A Learjet 60, described as a private aircraft
Route
Departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas, bound for North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas, according to the FAA
On board
Nine people; all were killed
On the ground
No injuries reported; about 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community evacuated
Investigation
Led by the NTSB with help from the FAA; the cause has not been determined

What happened

The jet crashed in a residential area near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North shortly before 1 p.m. on Sunday, triggering a fiery explosion and a large emergency response, CBS12 reported. Palm Beach County Fire Rescue said an explosion reported in the area was "related to an aircraft incident," according to NBC6. The crash left a crater in the roadway, scattered debris nearby and broke a water main, according to CBS12.

"It was a cataclysmic plane crash," Gov. Ron DeSantis told reporters at the scene, the Associated Press reported. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called it a "horrific tragedy."

According to air traffic control recordings reported by the AP, the pilot reported icing and turbulence and asked to climb 2,000 feet before the plane went into a steep dive and the pilot declared an emergency. Heavy rain was falling in the area at the time, and a flood watch was in effect. Investigators have not said what caused the crash.

The victims

As of Monday morning, authorities had not publicly released the names of the nine people who died, CBS12 reported. Bishop Manuel de Jesús Rodríguez of the Diocese of Palm Beach said he was praying for the victims' families and for residents affected by the crash, and a memorial Mass for the victims was scheduled for 10 a.m. Monday at Our Lady Queen of Peace Cemetery in Royal Palm Beach.

Road closures: Military Trail

  • Military Trail is closed between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, according to Palm Beach County officials and CBS12.
  • Officials have said the closure could last for weeks while investigators work at the scene and the damaged roadway is assessed.
  • Drivers heading east from Wellington toward Lake Worth Beach, Greenacres or West Palm Beach should plan alternate routes and allow extra time, especially with heavy rain and flooded roads in parts of the county.

Evacuations and closures

  • About 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community were evacuated.
  • Evacuees were moved to the Greenacres Community Center, 501 Swain Blvd., according to an update Monday reported by CBS12.
  • John I. Leonard High School, near the crash scene, was closed Monday as emergency crews continued their work.
  • At least one nearby business, Señor Lechón, said it was affected by the crash and didn't know when it would reopen; it said employees and customers who were there were safe.

The investigation

The NTSB is leading the investigation, with the Federal Aviation Administration assisting. The NTSB was scheduled to give an update at 1 p.m. Monday in West Palm Beach. The NTSB, not local agencies, determines the probable cause of civil aviation crashes, and that process typically takes many months. Until investigators release findings, any explanation of what caused the crash is speculation.

What it means for Wellington residents

The crash site is east of Wellington, along a corridor many Wellington residents use to reach Greenacres, Lake Worth Beach and West Palm Beach. If your commute uses Military Trail between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, expect a long-term closure. Check Palm Beach County and Florida 511 traffic updates before you drive.

How to stay informed

For official updates, follow Palm Beach County Fire Rescue, Palm Beach County government and the NTSB. Avoid the area so emergency crews and investigators can work. We'll keep this page updated with verified facts as they're released.
