    How to Get a Business Tax Receipt in Wellington, FL: Home Businesses, Rentals, Fees and Exemptions

    By Ben SiegelPublished November 19, 20267 min read

    Starting a business in Wellington, Florida, even a one-person business run from your kitchen table? You'll need a Wellington Business Tax Receipt (BTR) and a Palm Beach County BTR. Renting out a home requires one too, and short-term rentals need an extra permit. Here's how the process works, what it costs, the rules for home-based businesses, who qualifies for an exemption, and the renewal deadline that adds penalties after Sept. 30.

    Information is from the Village of Wellington's Business Tax Receipts, Home-Based Businesses, Commercial Businesses, Rental Properties, Exemptions, Changes to My Business and Renewing My Business Tax Receipt pages and its Business Tax and Vacation Rentals FAQs, checked Nov. 19, 2026. Fees and rules can change, so confirm with the Business Tax office.

    Who needs a Business Tax Receipt?

    Wellington's Code of Ordinances (Article III) requires a local BTR before you engage in any business in the village. That includes:

    • Commercial businesses with a storefront, office or branch in Wellington
    • Home-based businesses, even one-person companies
    • Rental properties: owners who rent a home in Wellington for any length of time

    The village says anyone who provides merchandise, entertainment or services to the public needs a BTR from both Wellington and Palm Beach County. If you run two or more separate lines of business, you need a BTR for each. Despite the name, a BTR is a tax receipt, not a professional license. Contractors, cosmetologists and other regulated trades also need their state license.

    How to get your Wellington BTR, step by step

    1. Register your business name with the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) if you're using a fictitious name ("doing business as") or forming a company.
    2. Request the right application from the village's Business Tax Forms page: Commercial Business, Home-Based Business or Rental Property.
    3. Gather your documents (checklists below), including the Palm Beach County BTR application, which is submitted as part of Wellington's.
    4. Submit everything together by email to BTR@wellingtonfl.gov, by mail, or in person at Village Hall, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The village asks that all documents go in one email or envelope to avoid delays.
    5. Zoning review: Planning and Zoning checks that your business is allowed at that address. If it isn't, the village says you'll need to move to a properly zoned location.
    6. Pay the invoice. Fees are invoiced after the application is processed.
    7. Register with the county. Wellington signs off on your county application and sends it back, but you must file it yourself with the Palm Beach County Tax Collector's business tax office, (561) 355-2264.
    8. Post your BTR in a conspicuous place at your business.

    What it costs

    According to the village's FAQ, Wellington's business tax ranges from $100 to $2,500, depending on the business category. Some categories are based on the number of employees, seats, rental units, vending machines or inventory. The full schedule is in Ordinance 2012-14. Rental property BTRs also carry administration, zoning review and per-unit fees. Palm Beach County charges its own separate business tax.

    Rules for home-based businesses

    Under Wellington's Land Development Regulations (Section 6.2.2.B.12), a home business must stay secondary to living there:

    • 15% limit: no more than 15% of the home's floor area can be used for the business.
    • Inside the house: work must be done in the main dwelling, not a shed, porch or carport. Instruction that has to happen outdoors, such as swim lessons, is allowed but shouldn't be visible from the street.
    • Employees: up to two employees or contractors who don't live there may work at the home. Remote employees who don't come to the house don't count.
    • No signs or advertised address: no outside sign, and the home address can't be advertised in any media, including online. Vehicle lettering is limited to what licensing rules require.
    • No outdoor storage: equipment and materials can't be stored or displayed outside, including in the driveway.
    • Normal traffic: parking can't exceed what a typical home would have, and business vehicles and trailers must be legally parked (not in the right-of-way, over a sidewalk or on grass). Commercial vehicles must follow the village's commercial vehicle rules; see our code compliance guide.

    Before you start, also check your HOA's rules, which may limit home businesses further.

    Renting out a home or running a vacation rental

    Wellington requires a BTR for any residential rental, long-term or short-term. Renters must also meet the village's definition of "family," which allows no more than four unrelated people living together as one household.

    Vacation rentals need more. Under Ordinance 2023-13, effective March 1, 2024, a home rented more than three times a year for 30 days or less needs a Vacation Rental (Special Use) Permit from the Planning, Zoning and Building Department before it can get a BTR. The village says it adopted the ordinance in response to a growing number of vacation rentals and problems reported with them. Key points:

    • One permit is required for each rental unit.
    • The permit is a one-time permit, but the BTR renews yearly. The permit doesn't transfer if the home is sold.
    • The responsible party must keep non-invasive noise and Wi-Fi monitoring and complete human trafficking prevention training.
    • Apply through the village's Customer Self Service portal or at Village Hall. Questions: vacationrentals@wellingtonfl.gov or (561) 791-4000.

    Rental BTRs don't qualify for the exemptions below.

    Who's exempt from the tax

    Under Florida law, these groups still need a Wellington BTR but pay no tax, with documentation:

    • Honorably discharged veterans and their unmarried widows or widowers, and spouses of active-duty service members who moved to Wellington on permanent change of station orders
    • People receiving public assistance or with income below 130% of the federal poverty level
    • Seniors 65 and older, people with disabilities who can't do manual labor, and widows with minor dependents who have no more than one employee and no more than $1,000 of their own capital in the business
    • Nonprofits (educational and charitable), which also don't need a county BTR
    • Charitable, religious, youth and civic groups making occasional sales or fundraising by members

    Renewing: don't miss Sept. 30

    All BTRs renew every year between July 1 and Sept. 30. You'll need current copies of any state and county licenses before the village will invoice you. Renew online through the Customer Self Service portal (accounts were reset Oct. 25, 2024, so you may need to create a new one), by mail or in person. Late renewals get automatic penalties:

    PaidPenalty
    October10%
    November15%
    December20%
    January or later25%
    Feb. 1 or laterPlus a $250 non-compliance fee

    If you haven't renewed this year, you're already in the 15% penalty window for November. Businesses that operate without paying within 150 days of notice also face a $250 penalty, court costs and collection fees.

    Changes: moving, changing your business name, selling the business or changing what you do all require a new application. Commercial moves and ownership changes also need a fire safety inspection first.

    Tips for new business owners

    • Check zoning first, before signing a lease. Call Planning and Zoning at (561) 791-4000.
    • Sales tax: if you'll collect it, register with the Florida Department of Revenue, (561) 640-2800.
    • Building out a space? You'll need permits and a certificate before your BTR is issued; see our building permits guide.
    • Get listed: once you're open, find local customers through the WellingtonList services directory.

    Two sides of the business tax

    Supporters say local business taxes help fund village services and let Wellington confirm that businesses are properly licensed and zoned, protecting neighborhoods and customers. Critics of local business taxes statewide argue they add paperwork and cost for very small businesses, and in the 2026 session Florida lawmakers considered bills (SB 122 and HB 103) to repeal local business taxes; both died in committee in March 2026. For now, the exemptions above ease the cost for veterans, seniors and low-income owners.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need a business license for a home business in Wellington, FL?

    Yes. Wellington requires a local Business Tax Receipt before engaging in any business in the village, including home-based and one-person businesses, plus a Palm Beach County Business Tax Receipt.

    How much is a Wellington Business Tax Receipt?

    Wellington's business tax ranges from $100 to $2,500 a year depending on the business category, according to the village. Some categories are based on employees, seats, units or inventory.

    What are the rules for home-based businesses in Wellington?

    No more than 15% of the home can be used for business, work must be inside the main dwelling, up to two non-resident employees may work there, and no signs, advertised address or outdoor storage of equipment are allowed.

    When do Wellington Business Tax Receipts renew?

    Every year between July 1 and Sept. 30. Late renewals get a 10% penalty in October, rising to 15%, 20% and 25%, with an added $250 non-compliance fee from Feb. 1.

    Do vacation rentals need a permit in Wellington?

    Yes. Under Ordinance 2023-13, effective March 1, 2024, homes rented more than three times a year for 30 days or less need a Vacation Rental (Special Use) Permit, plus an annual Business Tax Receipt.
