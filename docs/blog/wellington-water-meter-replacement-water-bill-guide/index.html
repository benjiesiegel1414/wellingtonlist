    Wellington Is Replacing Water Meters: What to Expect, Plus How Your Water Bill Works

    By Ben SiegelPublished November 22, 20266 min read

    If a uniformed technician knocks on your door to swap out your water meter, it's real. The Village of Wellington is replacing water meters across its utility system with new ones that send readings wirelessly, a project that started in June 2026 and is expected to take 12 to 18 months. Here's what happens on installation day, how to tell a real installer from a scam, and a plain-English guide to your Wellington water bill: how it's calculated, why it spikes, and every way to pay.

    Information is from the Village of Wellington's Water Meter Replacement Program page and FAQ, its Billing Information and Pay My Water Bill pages, and its Utility Bill FAQ, checked Nov. 22, 2026. Rates change, so see the village's Current Utility Rates and Fees document for today's numbers.

    Why the village is replacing meters

    The new meters send readings wirelessly to the utility office for billing and service alerts. The village gives two reasons for the switch:

    • Newer technology at no added cost. The village says it planned for the replacement, so there's no direct charge on customer bills for the new meter.
    • Fewer truck trips. Today, utility vehicles drive routes to collect readings. Wireless readings end those drive-by trips, which the village says cuts its carbon footprint.

    Initial replacements began in June 2026, with village-wide installation following in late summer. At a 12- to 18-month pace, work would wrap up between mid-2027 and the end of 2027.

    What to expect on installation day

    StepWhat happens
    BeforeYou'll get a notice in the mail and a door hanger before crews reach your area. You don't need to schedule anything.
    ArrivalA technician knocks to let you know they're starting. You don't have to be home.
    The swapWater is off for roughly 15 to 30 minutes while the meter in the box near the street is changed.
    AfterRun a bathtub faucet or outside spigot for 2 to 3 minutes to clear air from the line. Cloudy water right after the swap is from air and should clear.

    How you can help: clear plants, mulch and landscaping from around your meter box so crews can reach it. If you're planting a new hedge for Wellington's Dec. 31 hedge deadline, keep it off the meter box.

    How to spot a real installer

    Utility work is a common cover for door-to-door scams, so it's worth knowing what the real thing looks like. According to the village, Envocore technicians will:

    • Wear a uniform and carry valid photo ID
    • Drive a clearly marked vehicle with village-endorsed signage
    • Work Monday through Friday, not weekends
    • Work at the meter box outside. The village's instructions don't call for anyone to enter your home.

    There's no charge for the new meter, so no one should ask you for payment at the door. If something feels off, call Wellington Utilities at (561) 791-4010 to confirm before letting anyone onto your property. If your property is damaged during the work, call Envocore at (888) 632-9628, weekdays 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.

    How your Wellington water bill is calculated

    Meters are read every month. Your bill has up to three parts:

    Part of the billHow it's set
    Base rate (fixed monthly service charge)Based on your meter size. Charged every month, even with zero usage.
    Water and sewer usageBased on the gallons that pass through your meter that month.
    Solid wasteA collection and disposal charge may also appear on the bill.

    Snowbirds, take note: you'll get a bill every month even when the house is empty. The village says base fees cover the cost of keeping service available at your address, whether or not you use it.

    Sewer cap: the sewer portion stops being charged once your water use passes 15,000 gallons in a billing cycle, according to the village. There's no credit for filling a pool, but that cap can soften the hit.

    Why your bill jumped

    The village says the most common causes of a high bill are leaky plumbing fixtures and too much irrigation. Quick checks:

    • Toilets: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows up in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.
    • Meter test: turn off every faucet and appliance, then look at the meter. If it's still moving, you likely have a leak. A local plumber can track it down.
    • Sprinklers: check the timer after power outages, and look for broken heads. Watering is also limited by day and banned from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.; see the lawn watering rules.

    The toilet and meter checks are standard leak tests, not village requirements. Once wireless meters are in, the village says they'll also send "important service notifications" to the utility office; it hasn't detailed what those will include.

    Every way to pay

    MethodDetails
    OnlineOne-time payment or auto pay through the Pay My Water Bill page at wellingtonfl.gov
    PhoneAutomated system at (561) 791-4010
    MailWellington Utilities, P.O. Box 31632, Tampa, FL 33631-3632
    In person or drop boxVillage Hall, 12300 Forest Hill Blvd.

    Watch out for bank bill-pay. If you pay through your bank's online bill payment service, the village warns that the bank often mails a paper check to the Tampa P.O. box, so it can take much longer to post than you'd expect. Pay early, or use the village's own online system.

    Your Customer ID and account number are at the top of your bill: the Customer ID is to the left of the dash and the account number is to the right.

    Starting or closing an account

    • New account: email customerservice@wellingtonfl.gov for the application and checklist, then sign and email everything back.
    • Closing as a tenant: email a request with a copy of your photo ID and the date you want service out of your name.
    • Closing as an owner: email a copy of the warranty deed showing the property transfer, your photo ID and the end date.

    Not every Wellington address is a village customer. Some neighborhoods east of State Road 7 are served by Palm Beach County Utilities instead. Check your bill to see who your provider is; details are in our Wellington water utility guide.

    Two views on wireless meters

    Utilities like wireless meters because they cut labor and truck costs and can flag problems sooner. Some residents in other communities have raised concerns about smart meters, including billing changes when an old, slow meter is replaced with an accurate one, and questions about data and radio signals. The village says there's no direct charge for the new meters. If your usage reading changes noticeably after the swap, call (561) 791-4010 and ask for a review.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Wellington replacing water meters?

    Yes. The Village of Wellington began replacing water meters in June 2026 with meters that send readings wirelessly. The village expects the project to take 12 to 18 months.

    Do I have to pay for the new water meter in Wellington?

    No. The village says there is no direct charge on your bill for the meter replacement.

    How long will my water be off during the meter replacement?

    About 15 to 30 minutes. You don't need to be home. Afterward, run a tub faucet or outside spigot for 2 to 3 minutes to clear air from the line.

    Why do I get a Wellington water bill when I'm not using water?

    The village charges a fixed monthly base fee, set by meter size, to keep service available at your address regardless of usage or occupancy.

    How do I pay my Wellington water bill?

    Pay online or set up auto pay through the Pay My Water Bill page at wellingtonfl.gov, by phone at 561-791-4010, by mail to the Tampa P.O. box on your bill, or in person or by drop box at Village Hall, 12300 Forest Hill Blvd.
