Living in Wellington, FL: The Honest Pros and Cons

By Wellington List staffPublished October 3, 20266 min read
Advertisement Sponsor WellingtonList.comPromote your local business or service here, in front of Wellington residents every day. Become a sponsor

Thinking about moving to Wellington, Florida? This village in western Palm Beach County has a reputation for top schools, big parks, safe neighborhoods and a world-famous horse scene. It also has real trade-offs: winter traffic, summer storms and a long drive to the beach. Here is an honest look at what living in Wellington is actually like.

Wellington at a glance

December 31, 1995, as a village
County

The pros of living in Wellington

1. Strong schools

Schools are the number one reason many families choose Wellington. Several of the village's public elementary and middle schools are among the highest rated in Palm Beach County, and there are charter and private options too. See our full guide to schools in Wellington.

2. Parks, lakes and green space everywhere

Wellington is a planned community, and it shows. Neighborhoods are built around lakes and canals, sidewalks and bike paths connect much of the village, and there are large parks and nature preserves within a few minutes of most homes. Village Park, Peaceful Waters Sanctuary and the Wellington Environmental Preserve are local favorites. Here is our guide to the best parks in Wellington.

3. A real community feel

For a town of more than 60,000 people, Wellington feels surprisingly close knit. Town Center hosts concerts, food truck nights and holiday events at the Wellington Amphitheater, youth sports leagues are huge, and you will run into neighbors at the same restaurants every week.

4. The equestrian lifestyle

Every winter, Wellington becomes the center of the horse world, with the Winter Equestrian Festival, the Adequan Global Dressage Festival and Sunday polo at the National Polo Center. Even if you never ride, the season brings world-class events, restaurants and energy to town, and many of the shows are free or inexpensive to watch.

5. Space and a quieter pace

Compared with the coast, Wellington lots tend to be bigger, the streets are quieter, and there are options from townhomes to estates and horse farms. It feels suburban without being crowded.

6. Everyday convenience

Groceries, doctors, Wellington Regional Medical Center, The Mall at Wellington Green and plenty of restaurants are all within the village, so most errands stay local. Palm Beach International Airport is roughly a 20 to 30 minute drive.

The cons of living in Wellington

1. Season traffic

From January through April, the equestrian season and winter residents add a lot of cars. Roads around South Shore Blvd, Pierson Road, Forest Hill Blvd and State Road 7 get busy, especially on weekends and show nights.

2. Far from the beach

Wellington is inland. Getting to the beach in Palm Beach or Lake Worth Beach typically means a 30 to 45 minute drive depending on traffic, which is a deal breaker for some people moving to Florida.

3. Cost of living

Home prices in Wellington run above the Palm Beach County average, especially in the most popular school zones and gated communities, and the equestrian estates are in a different price universe entirely. Budget for HOA fees in many neighborhoods, plus Florida homeowners insurance, which has risen sharply statewide.

4. HOAs and rules

Most Wellington neighborhoods have homeowners associations, and the village itself has detailed codes on everything from landscaping to signage. That keeps the community looking sharp, but it is not for everyone.

5. Summer heat, rain and hurricanes

Summers are hot, humid and stormy, with afternoon downpours most days from about June through September. Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. Check flood zones and wind-mitigation features before buying, and have a storm plan.

6. Limited nightlife

Wellington has good restaurants and a few lively bars, but it is a family town. For clubs or a big night out, most people head to West Palm Beach.

Who Wellington is best for

  • Families who want strong schools, safe neighborhoods and youth sports.
  • Equestrians and horse lovers, obviously.
  • Remote workers and retirees who want space, parks and a calmer pace while staying near West Palm Beach.

It is a tougher fit if you want walkable city living, a beach lifestyle or a lot of nightlife.

Getting to know the area

Before you buy or rent, spend a weekday morning and a Saturday in season driving the roads you would use every day, check your exact address for school zones, and walk the parks near the neighborhoods you like. Browse our Wellington directory for local restaurants, services and real estate offices, and keep up with what is being built next, like Village Landing at K-Park and the Mall at Wellington Green redevelopment.

Frequently asked questions

Is Wellington, FL a good place to live?

Wellington is popular with families for its highly rated schools, parks, safe neighborhoods and community events, and it is world famous for equestrian sports. The main trade-offs are winter season traffic, higher home prices than the county average, HOAs, and a 30 to 45 minute drive to the beach.

How far is Wellington, FL from the beach?

Wellington is inland in western Palm Beach County. Most residents drive 30 to 45 minutes to reach the beach in Palm Beach or Lake Worth Beach, depending on traffic.

What is the population of Wellington, FL?

Wellington had 61,637 residents at the 2020 Census.

When is traffic worst in Wellington?

Traffic is heaviest from January through April, during the equestrian season, especially around South Shore Blvd, Pierson Road, Forest Hill Blvd and State Road 7.
