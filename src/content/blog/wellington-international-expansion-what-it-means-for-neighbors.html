Wellington International, home of the Winter Equestrian Festival, is about to look very different. A new 98-acre section called Wellington International South is opening this fall, bringing the showgrounds to 215 acres and moving dressage next door to the jumpers for the first time. The trade-off that made it possible is still one of the most debated decisions in recent Wellington history: luxury homes on land that used to be part of the Equestrian Preserve. Here's what's being built, how the deal works and what each side has said.

The expansion at a glance

What's new
Wellington International South, a 98-acre addition that brings the venue to 215 acres
What's in it
A 3,000-seat international arena, an 84,000-square-foot covered arena, a grass derby field, new rings, five permanent barns and a 1,500-seat covered grandstand
When
Soft launch in fall 2026, full debut in the 2027 season
The trade
About 96 acres at Equestrian Village, the current dressage site on South Shore Boulevard, were removed from the Equestrian Preserve for a residential community
The safeguard
Homes can't be built on the Equestrian Village land until the reconfigured horse show is delivered, by the end of 2028

How the deal came together

In February 2024, after four days and roughly 22 hours of testimony, the Village Council approved the land-use changes behind the plan, the Chronicle of the Horse reported. Developer Wellington Lifestyle Partners got approval to:

  • Rezone land south of the existing Wellington International grounds for commercial equestrian use, so dressage and the hunters and jumpers can share one connected property. That vote was unanimous.
  • Remove about 96 acres at Equestrian Village from the Equestrian Preserve so it can become a residential community. That vote was 4-1, with then-Vice Mayor Michael Napoleone voting no.

In March 2024, the council approved the detailed showgrounds plan 4-0, the Town-Crier reported. The key condition: the developers must deliver the reconfigured horse show by the end of 2028, or they can't build homes on the Equestrian Village land.

What's being built

According to Wellington International's June 2026 announcement, the new south section will be the future home of the Global Dressage Festival and will also host FEI show jumping, hunter derbies and other events. Plans include:

  • A CDI international arena for up to 3,000 spectators and an 84,000-square-foot covered arena
  • Four more hunter/jumper rings, two national dressage rings and a grass derby field with a tailgating area
  • Five permanent 60-stall barns, which Dressage News reported will hold 317 stalls in all
  • A permanent covered grandstand seating about 1,500, plus a new show office, food and retail areas
  • Golf cart shuttles between the north and south properties and a new equestrian trail linking South Shore Boulevard to preserve trails

"This project represents the next chapter for equestrian sports in Wellington," Wellington International President Michael Stone said in the announcement.

The case for the expansion

  • Keeping Wellington on top. Many riders and trainers testified in 2024 that the grounds needed upgrades to keep up with newer venues like the World Equestrian Center in Ocala. Then-Councilman Michael Drahos said the vote was about the industry's long-term survival.
  • All disciplines in one place. Consolidating dressage with the Winter Equestrian Festival means one campus, new barns and permanent seating instead of two separate show sites.
  • The economy. Supporters point to the tax base and the winter visitors, horses and businesses the show season brings to Wellington. Then-Mayor Anne Gerwig said the council had to look at the decision "very holistically," including the tax base and the future of the horse show.
  • Built-in protection. The homes can't come until the show is delivered. Even Napoleone, who voted against removing the preserve land, later said of the final plan, "we're getting all the pieces of a world-class horse show."

The case for concern

  • The Equestrian Preserve. Some residents argued that taking land out of the preserve could be the first step toward changing Wellington's horse-country character. Napoleone argued the rezoning mainly served a private residential resort and said, "We're not a vacation resort destination."
  • Traffic and construction. Neighbors raised concerns about more traffic on South Shore Boulevard and Pierson Road and years of construction in the equestrian area.
  • Neighborhood security. Residents of Equestrian Club Estates asked for clearer plans to keep show visitors out of their neighborhood.
  • An alternative that wasn't taken. Before the vote, a group of local property owners offered $25 million for the Equestrian Village land and said they would keep it for horse shows. The developer said it wasn't interested in selling.

What it means for neighbors this season

  • Wellington International lists its main entrance at 3400 Equestrian Club Drive and competitor entrances on Pierson Road and Gene Mische Way, with thousands of car and golf cart spaces planned for the expansion.
  • Equestrian Village on South Shore Boulevard is still in use for now. The housing can't be built until the new show facilities are delivered.
