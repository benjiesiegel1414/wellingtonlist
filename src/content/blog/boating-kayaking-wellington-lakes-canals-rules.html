Wellington is full of water: Lake Wellington behind the Community Center, dozens of neighborhood lakes, and miles of canals that keep the village drained. Can you kayak them? Take a jon boat out? Ride a jet ski? Here are the rules for boating, kayaking and paddling in Wellington, Florida, from the village's Public Works FAQ and Florida law, plus where to launch, the permit you need for the boat ramp, rowing lessons, and how to stay safe around alligators.

What's allowed on Wellington's water

The village's Public Works FAQ spells out the rules from its Code of Ordinances:

WatercraftAllowed on public lakes and canals?
Jet skis, wave runners and other personal watercraftNo. Expressly prohibited on any publicly owned water body, lake or canal in Wellington
Canoes and kayaksYes
Paddleboats and rowboatsYes
Boats with electric motorsYes, with no limit on motor power, but they must stay at no-wake speed
Gas-powered boatsThe FAQ lists only non-motorized and electric-powered boats as allowed; check with the village before launching anything else

No wake means slow. The village says all boats must keep "no wake" speeds on Wellington waterways, which it puts at about 5 knots. Wakes erode canal banks, which the village spends money each year to reslope and resod.

The full rules are in Wellington's Code of Ordinances. Lakes inside private communities may have their own HOA rules, so check those before you launch from a backyard.

Learn to row on Lake Wellington

The Florida Rowing Center has operated since 1985 from the village boathouse at 12185 Ken Adams Way, next to the Wellington Community Center. The village describes it as specializing in individual sculling instruction for all ages and abilities, "from Olympians to first-timers." Its youth summer camp (ages 12 to 18, weekdays 9 a.m. to noon) cost $295 a week in 2026, and the only prerequisite was reasonable swimming skills. Register through the Florida Rowing Center's website.

Florida rules that still apply

Wellington's waterways are small, but state boating law still applies. According to FWC:

  • Life jackets: every vessel, including canoes and kayaks, must carry a wearable, U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket for each person, in good condition and within easy reach. Children under 6 must wear one while underway on a vessel under 26 feet.
  • Registration: non-motorized canoes, kayaks, rowing shells and sculls don't need to be registered, and neither do other non-motorized boats under 16 feet. Motorized boats, including electric ones, generally must be registered through the Tax Collector.
  • Boater education: anyone born on or after Jan. 1, 1988, who operates a boat with a motor of 10 horsepower or more needs a boating safety education ID card.
  • After dark: boats must show navigation lights between sunset and sunrise. The boat ramp closes at sunset anyway.

Alligators and wildlife

Every body of fresh water in Florida can have alligators, and Wellington's lakes and canals are no exception. FWC's advice:

  • Keep your distance if you see one, on land or from your boat.
  • Keep pets on a leash and away from the water's edge. FWC notes pets can resemble an alligator's natural prey.
  • Swim only in designated swimming areas, during daylight. Alligators are most active between dusk and dawn. Canals and retention lakes aren't swimming areas; for swimming, see our swim lessons and pools guide.
  • Never feed an alligator. FWC says it's illegal and dangerous.
  • If you believe an alligator poses a threat, call FWC's Nuisance Alligator Hotline at 866-FWC-GATOR (866-392-4286).

The village doesn't remove live animals; its Public Works FAQ refers residents to FWC's regional office at (561) 625-5122 or a private trapper. The village's Living with Wildlife page links to FWC resources on avoiding conflicts.

Report problems on the water

Seen heavy algae, weeds choking a canal, or illegal dumping? Report it through SeeClickFix Wellington or call (561) 791-4000. See our SeeClickFix how-to. Canals and swales are part of the drainage system, so keep yard waste and grass clippings out of them. See our flooding and drainage explainer.

Watch the Holiday Boat Parade

Want to see Lake Wellington at its liveliest? The village's free Holiday Boat Parade is Saturday, Dec. 12, 2026, from 6:30 to 8 p.m. About 40 decorated boats pass behind the Community Center, with food trucks and holiday market vendors on the Town Center Promenade. Bring your own seating. The village says boaters interested in joining should contact the organizer listed on its Holiday Boat Parade page. More in our holiday events guide.

Should the rules loosen?

Some residents with waterfront homes would like more freedom on the water, pointing out that personal watercraft are common on lakes elsewhere in Florida. Supporters of Wellington's rules say the ban and no-wake limit keep canals quiet, protect banks from erosion, and keep paddlers, rowers and anglers safe on narrow waterways that double as drainage channels. For now, Wellington's water is built for paddles, oars and quiet electric motors.
