Updated Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, afternoon: Added new details from the NTSB's 1 p.m. news conference, including the flight's final minutes, evidence the plane may have begun breaking apart before impact, and a correction: investigators said one person on the ground suffered a leg injury. This is a developing story, and we'll keep updating it with verified information.

A Learjet 60 carrying nine people crashed near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in the Greenacres area of Palm Beach County on Sunday afternoon, Nov. 1, 2026, killing everyone on board. The business jet came down in a residential area near a busy roadway, east of Wellington, leaving a crater in the road and forcing about 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community to evacuate. Investigators said one person on the ground, a man in a vehicle leaving the area, suffered a leg injury. Here is what has been confirmed so far, and what it means for getting around the area.

What we know

When
Sunday, Nov. 1, 2026, at about 12:51 p.m., according to the NTSB
Where
Near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North in the Greenacres area, about 7 miles southwest of West Palm Beach
Aircraft
A Learjet 60 operated by Aurora Aviation, doing business as Upper Jets, according to the NTSB
Route
Departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas at about 10:37 a.m., bound for North Eleuthera in the Bahamas
On board
Nine people (two flight crew and seven passengers); all were killed
On the ground
One man in a vehicle suffered a leg injury, according to the NTSB; about 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community were evacuated
Investigation
Led by the NTSB with help from the FAA; weather is "a major focus," but the cause has not been determined

What happened

The jet crashed in a residential area near Military Trail and 10th Avenue North shortly before 1 p.m. on Sunday, triggering a fiery explosion and a large emergency response, CBS12 reported. Palm Beach County Fire Rescue said an explosion reported in the area was "related to an aircraft incident," according to NBC6. The crash left a crater in the roadway, scattered debris nearby and broke a water main, according to CBS12.

"It was a cataclysmic plane crash," Gov. Ron DeSantis told reporters at the scene, the Associated Press reported. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called it a "horrific tragedy."

The flight's final minutes, according to the NTSB

At a news conference Monday, NTSB Member Tom Chapman laid out a preliminary timeline, as reported by CBS12 and WPTV:

  • About 10:37 a.m.: The Learjet departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport with two crew members and seven passengers.
  • About 12:47 p.m.: Flying at 35,000 feet, the crew told air traffic control they were encountering light clear ice, which they considered manageable, and moderate turbulence, with altitude deviations of about 700 feet. They reported an outside temperature of minus 53 degrees Fahrenheit.
  • The crew then reported precipitation and asked to climb to 37,000 feet. Air traffic control approved the request.
  • "Less than 30 seconds later, a transmission from the airplane announced the descent, followed by a mayday call," Chapman said.
  • About 12:48 p.m.: A transmission said the plane was "in an overspeed condition descending."
  • About 12:51 p.m.: The plane crashed near Greenacres and Palm Springs.

Heavy rain was falling in the area at the time, and a flood watch was in effect.

The victims

As of Monday afternoon, authorities had not released a full list of the nine people who died. Bishop Manuel de Jesús Rodríguez of the Diocese of Palm Beach said he was praying for the victims' families and for residents affected by the crash, and a memorial Mass for the victims was scheduled for 10 a.m. Monday at Our Lady Queen of Peace Cemetery in Royal Palm Beach.

Road closures: Military Trail

  • Military Trail is closed between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, according to Palm Beach County officials and CBS12.
  • Officials have said the closure could last for weeks while investigators work at the scene and the damaged roadway is assessed.
  • Drivers heading east from Wellington toward Lake Worth Beach, Greenacres or West Palm Beach should plan alternate routes and allow extra time, especially with heavy rain and flooded roads in parts of the county.

Evacuations and closures

  • About 600 residents of a nearby mobile home community were evacuated.
  • Evacuees were moved to the Greenacres Community Center, 501 Swain Blvd., according to an update Monday reported by CBS12.
  • John I. Leonard High School, near the crash scene, was closed Monday as emergency crews continued their work.
  • At least one nearby business, Señor Lechón, said it was affected by the crash and didn't know when it would reopen; it said employees and customers who were there were safe.

The investigation

  • Weather is a focus. "The weather conditions are a major focus of the investigation," Chapman said, adding that investigators still need to determine conditions at the plane's cruising altitude. The NTSB has not confirmed whether the plane flew through an active thunderstorm.
  • Possible breakup before impact. "We do have some evidence that there was some breakup of the aircraft before impact," Chapman said, while cautioning that the investigation is in its early stages and recovered pieces haven't all been identified.
  • A large debris field. Debris covers an area of roughly half a mile by four-tenths of a mile, including ball fields, businesses and homes, according to Investigator-in-Charge Timothy Monville.
  • Cockpit voice recorder. Maintenance records indicate one was installed, but investigators are still working to confirm it was aboard and whether it can be recovered. Water in the impact crater is complicating access to the main wreckage.
  • The team. About 20 NTSB investigators, including specialists in aircraft systems and structures, recorders, weather and air traffic control, are working with the FAA and the Learjet manufacturer. Work at the scene is expected to take several days.

The NTSB does not determine the probable cause at the scene, and that process typically takes many months. Until investigators release findings, any explanation of what caused the crash is speculation.

Have information? The NTSB is asking witnesses with information, photos or video to email witness@ntsb.gov and asks anyone who finds debris to leave it in place and contact authorities.

What it means for Wellington residents

The crash site is east of Wellington, along a corridor many Wellington residents use to reach Greenacres, Lake Worth Beach and West Palm Beach. If your commute uses Military Trail between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, expect a long-term closure. Check Palm Beach County and Florida 511 traffic updates before you drive.

How to stay informed

For official updates, follow Palm Beach County Fire Rescue, Palm Beach County government and the NTSB. Avoid the area so emergency crews and investigators can work. We'll keep this page updated with verified facts as they're released.
