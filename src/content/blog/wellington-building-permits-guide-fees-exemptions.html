Planning a renovation, fence, roof or addition in Wellington, Florida? Most projects need a building permit from the village, and Wellington processes permits entirely online. A new state law, House Bill 803, also lets owners of single-family homes apply for an exemption for some small projects under $7,500 starting July 1, 2026. Here's how the permit process works step by step, what permits cost, the exemption rules and the costly mistakes to avoid.

How the permit process works

Wellington's Building Division only processes permits electronically. Here's the process the village lays out:

  1. Submit the application by email. Send your completed building permit application to the Building Division at buildingdocs@wellingtonfl.gov (permit forms are on the village's Building Forms page). A permit technician enters it into the system.
  2. Get your ProjectDox login. First-time users get an email with a temporary password within about a minute of the application being entered. The contractor's registered email is used for the login, and permit runners must register too.
  3. Upload plans. In ProjectDox, upload files to the Plans, Support Documents or Product Approvals folders. Plan pages must be uploaded individually and digitally signed, and the village has specific file-naming rules. First-timers are asked to call (561) 753-2430 or (561) 363-7907 to make sure the upload worked.
  4. Pay fees in CSS, then return to ProjectDox and submit the task for review.
  5. Answer corrections. If reviewers need changes, you'll get an email. Respond to each unresolved comment, upload revised files and resubmit.
  6. Pay any remaining fees. County impact fees, if owed, must be paid in person by check or cash.
  7. Schedule inspections once the permit is issued.

ProjectDox works in Edge, Chrome, Firefox and Safari, but it uses pop-up windows, so allow pop-ups for the site.

What a permit costs

Wellington calculates building permit fees from the project's total value, including labor, materials, design, overhead and profit (but not land). The village's fee schedule uses these tiers:

Project valueFee
$0.01 to $7,500$150 minimum fee
$7,500.01 to $100,0002.00% of construction value, plus
$100,000.01 to $500,0001.75%, plus
$500,000.01 to $1.5 million1.50%, plus
Over $1.5 million1.25%

Added on top:

  • Plan review fee: 50% of the permit fee, with a $75 minimum, paid when you apply.
  • State surcharges: 1% (Department of Business and Professional Regulation) and 1.5% (Building Code Administrators and Inspectors Fund) of the permit cost, each with a $2 minimum.

Rough example: for a $20,000 project, reading the tiers as a $150 base plus 2% of the value above $7,500, the permit fee would be about $400, plus a $200 plan review fee and roughly $10 in state surcharges, or about $610 in all. That's our estimate; the Building Official sets the final valuation and fee.

The new $7,500 exemption (House Bill 803)

Since July 1, 2026, a Florida law lets owners of single-family homes, or their contractors, apply for an exemption from building permits for certain residential work valued under $7,500.

It does NOT apply to:

  • Electrical, gas, plumbing, mechanical or structural work
  • Pool barriers
  • Projects partly or fully in a flood hazard area
  • Projects split into smaller pieces to avoid permitting

Fences may qualify if they're under 3 feet tall, aren't a pool barrier, or are repairs that don't touch or change the posts.

How to apply: you can't just skip the permit; you must apply for the exemption. Email the village's House Bill 803 Exemption Form to buildingdocs@wellingtonfl.gov with a signed proposal, estimate, invoice or contract showing the value; a scope of work; a survey for any work outside the house; and a sketch or plan. The village reviews it within four business days, and there's currently no charge. If staff decide you need a permit, you'll have to apply for one.

The catch: exempt projects don't get village inspections, but they still must meet the Florida Building Code, Wellington zoning and floodplain rules, HOA requirements and contractor licensing laws, and the village says it has no legal responsibility for work done under the exemption. The village also notes the law mostly covers small non-trade projects Wellington never required permits for anyway.

Tips before you start

  • Check your HOA first. Many Wellington communities need architectural approval before the village permit.
  • Hire licensed contractors and let them pull the permit. The contractor's email is used for ProjectDox.
  • Include everything up front. Leaving work off the application can mean a separate permit with extra fees.
  • Be ready for inspections. Failed or not-ready inspections cost $60 and up each time.
  • Selling soon? Unpermitted work can complicate a sale; see our living in Wellington guide for local context and our contractors and handyman directory.
