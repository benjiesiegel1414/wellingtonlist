What happened on Longwood Green Drive

The village issued a precautionary boil water notice on Nov. 16 for the Longwood Green Drive community, covering all buildings at 11900, 11916, 11932 and 11964 Longwood Green Drive. Field services crews put up signs and left yellow door hangers with instructions. The village later posted an update saying the notice was lifted. Residents get a green door hanger when the water is confirmed safe to drink.

Why boil water notices happen

When a water main breaks or something else temporarily disrupts service, pressure can drop and the line could be exposed to contamination. As a precaution, the village places affected customers under a boil water notice until lab samples confirm the water is safe, which is typically about 48 hours, according to the village. "Precautionary" means contamination hasn't been confirmed; it's a safety step while testing is done.

What to do during a boil water notice

These steps come from the village's official boil water instructions:

Drinking water

  • Don't drink tap water. Use bottled water or tap water boiled at a rolling boil for one minute.
  • No power? Add 8 drops (1/8 teaspoon) of unscented household bleach per gallon of water and let it stand 30 minutes. If the water is cloudy, use 16 drops (1/4 teaspoon). It may smell or taste of chlorine but isn't toxic.

Ice and drinks

  • Ice made before the notice is fine to use. Throw out ice made after it.
  • Turn off ice makers and drinking fountains until the notice is lifted. Before restarting an ice maker, sanitize it with two teaspoons of household bleach in a gallon of water.
  • Replace water line filters if bacteria were found or debris has built up.

Handwashing, cooking and cleaning

  • Use bottled or boiled (and cooled) water for handwashing, then a hand sanitizer.
  • Don't use tap water to prepare food until the notice is lifted.
  • Clean food-contact surfaces with boiled or bottled water and a bleach solution.

When the notice is lifted

Watch for the green door hanger or a village update. It's a good idea to run your taps for a few minutes, toss any ice made during the notice and change refrigerator water filters if your manufacturer recommends it.

Lawn watering rules

Wellington follows South Florida Water Management District's year-round landscape irrigation rule, and the village waters its own property by zone, using reclaimed water where it's available. Under the district rule:

  • No landscape watering from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
  • Watering days depend on your address (odd or even) and whether your area is on a two- or three-day schedule. In three-day areas, even addresses may water Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday, and odd addresses Monday, Wednesday and Saturday. In two-day areas, even addresses water Thursday and Sunday, and odd addresses Wednesday and Saturday.
  • Exemptions include reclaimed water, hand watering with a self-canceling nozzle, new landscaping and system repairs.

Use the district's online lookup to confirm your days, and call Code Compliance at (561) 753-2560 with questions.

Fluoride: no longer added

Under a Florida law that took effect July 1, 2025 (Senate Bill 700), Wellington stopped adding fluoride to its drinking water. The village says its raw water naturally contains about 0.15 to 0.2 milligrams per liter of fluoride, which stays in the water after treatment. Supporters of the law said residents should decide for themselves about fluoride, while many dental groups recommend community fluoridation to prevent cavities. Families with questions about their children's fluoride needs can talk to a dentist or pediatrician; see our list of Wellington dentists.

About Wellington Utilities

The Wellington Utilities Department runs around the clock and serves about 56,000 residents in its service area with drinking water and wastewater service. It publishes an annual water quality report with detailed testing results. Treated water from its water reclamation program also feeds Peaceful Waters Sanctuary, a 30-acre constructed wetland where the village has recorded more than 150 bird species.

For drainage and flooding, which is handled by the Acme Improvement District rather than the water utility, see our flooding and drainage explainer.

Be ready before the next notice

  • Keep bottled water on hand, about a gallon per person per day for a few days. It doubles as hurricane prep.
  • Save the numbers above in your phone, especially the after-hours line.
  • Follow village news. Wellington posts boil water notices on its News Flash page at wellingtonfl.gov.
