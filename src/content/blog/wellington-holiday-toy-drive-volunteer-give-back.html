Want to give back in Wellington, Florida this holiday season? The village's Holiday Toy Drive is collecting new, unwrapped toys through Friday, Dec. 11, 2026, at five drop-off spots around town. Beyond the holidays, there are several ways to pitch in year-round, from Volunteer Wellington to adopting a street or planting a tree in someone's honor. Here's every option, with deadlines, costs and who to contact.

Wellington's Holiday Toy Drive

The village's annual toy drive runs from Sept. 3 through Friday, Dec. 11. The goal, in the village's words, is to make sure no child goes without a gift this holiday season. Donations should be new and unwrapped.

Easy ways to combine it with a night out: the remaining Thursday Nights before the deadline are Dec. 3, which is also the Holiday Cultural Celebration and Tree Lighting, and Dec. 10, the Holiday Bazaar. See our Wellington holiday events guide.

Missed the Dec. 11 deadline? Runners in the Wellington Holiday Jingle Bell Run 5K on Sunday, Dec. 20, at Village Park are encouraged to bring an unwrapped toy for the drive.

Who the toys go to

According to the village, the drive serves more than 500 families. Registered families "shop" for their own toys at a distribution event, and the program provides toys and gift cards for children up to 18 years old.

What to give: because the program covers kids through their teens, gifts for older children help too, and the village's own description mentions gift cards. Think sports equipment, art supplies, games, headphones and similar items along with toys for younger kids. That's our suggestion; the village's only stated requirement is that items be new and unwrapped.

Businesses: the village says partnership opportunities are still available. An event sponsorship is listed at $2,000, with logo placement on the event page, signage and flyer plus media and social recognition. Contact Community Services Director Jenifer Brito at (561) 753-2476.

Need help this season? Families who need assistance can call the village's Community Services office at (561) 791-4796 to ask about registering.

What about the food drive?

The village's Holiday Food Drive, which collects Thanksgiving staples for local families, ended Nov. 13 this year. It runs every fall and helps more than 250 families, the village says, so mark it for next September.

Volunteer Wellington

Volunteer Wellington is the village's program for residents who want to help with community events and services. The village pitches it as a way to learn new skills, gain work experience, meet people and see how local government works.

  • How to sign up: fill out the Volunteer Wellington Form on the village website.
  • Questions: call (561) 791-4102.
  • Good for: students who need service hours, retirees, and anyone new to town who wants to get connected. Students should confirm with their school that hours will count.

Adopt-A-Street

Groups, families and businesses can adopt a section of village roadway and keep it litter-free.

DetailWhat the village requires
CommitmentAt least four litter pickups a year
TermTwo years, with an option to renew
LimitUp to two street sections per sponsor
Cost$250 for for-profit groups (covers the signs); free for nonprofits
SuppliesSafety equipment, garbage bags and signs provided at no cost
ReportingTell Public Works the date of each pickup and how many bags you collected

Your group's name goes on a sign at each end of the section. Availability is limited: as of this week the village listed one open section, South Road from 140th Avenue to 40th Street, and it doesn't hold or reserve sections. To get the application, litter removal agreement and safety sheet, call Public Works at (561) 791-4117.

Plant a commemorative tree

A commemorative tree is a lasting way to honor a loved one or mark a birthday, wedding or other milestone.

  • Cost: $425 for a typical 10- to 12-foot tree, covering the tree, installation and a plaque. Larger trees or unusual species cost more.
  • Plaque wording: plaques for loved ones who have died should read "In honor of" or "In celebration of" and can't include dates. The village approves all plaques.
  • How: request the Commemorative Tree Form from Public Works at (561) 791-4117, then mail it with a check or money order payable to the Village of Wellington to Wellington, Attention: Public Works, 14001 Pierson Road, Wellington, FL 33414.

More ways to help

  • Community memorials and parks: the village's Donate/Contribute page lists the Patriot Memorial, Veterans Memorial, Flags of Wellington, the Wellington Dog Park and Scott's Place Playground, a barrier-free playground built with a donation from a Wellington family, among the places supported by community giving.
  • Report problems you see: flagging a broken sidewalk, a dead street light or illegal dumping is a small civic act. Our who to call in Wellington guide has every number.
  • Support seniors: the village hosts a free Senior Holiday Party on Dec. 15 for residents 50 and up; if you have an older neighbor, offer a ride. RSVP at (561) 753-2484.
  • Shop local: Thursday Nights in Wellington features small local vendors, a good place to buy gifts that keep money in the community.
