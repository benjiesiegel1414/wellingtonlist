Anyone who drives State Road 7 or Okeechobee Blvd. knows traffic out west is getting worse. Palm Beach County spent about $1.1 million on a Countywide Transportation Master Plan meant to coordinate road and transit projects through 2050. But in August 2026, county commissioners refused to adopt the 2,000-page plan, or even formally receive it, and sent staff back for something more concrete. Supporters call it a needed framework for working together. Critics say taxpayers paid for a plan that confirms what drivers already know. Here's what's in it and what each side has said.

The transportation plan at a glance

The plan
Palm Beach Countywide Transportation Master Plan (CTMP), a framework for transportation and mobility through 2050
Consultant
WSP; the contract has grown to about $1.1 million
What's in it
A database of nearly 2,000 projects and recommendations, and 21 priority corridors, including State Road 7 and Okeechobee Blvd.
Outreach
Community and stakeholder meetings from July 2025 to August 2026, including a western communities session in Belle Glade
Status
Not adopted. On Aug. 26, 2026, commissioners asked staff to return with project-level details on how much congestion each fix would relieve

What the plan proposes

The CTMP pulls the transportation plans of the county, its 39 municipalities and other partners into one decision-making framework, without creating a new agency, the Town-Crier reported. "We want to optimize first," WSP consultant Emily Ritzler said at a June meeting. "The good news is that you have a very robust transportation network."

Ideas in the draft recommendations include:

  • Flex lanes on roads with six or more lanes, whose use can change with congestion or transit demand
  • Roundabouts, strategic road widenings, new connections and elevated roadways
  • Adaptive traffic signals that adjust to real-time conditions
  • Expanded transit hubs, more frequent bus service and safer paths for bikes and pedestrians

The plan was built without cost estimates, to show what options exist rather than commit to specific projects.

The case for the plan

  • Traffic crosses borders. "Traffic moves like water. It goes where it goes, and people never know whose jurisdiction they're in — whose road they're on," County Vice Mayor Marci Woodward said. "My hope is that this gives us a framework to work together, because we all overlap."
  • A long-term blueprint. "No single government entity or agency can solve our transportation challenges today or in the future," said Royal Palm Beach Mayor Jeff Hmara, who suggested the county receive the plan but hold off on adopting it. "With continued partnership, the CTMP can become that durable, living and implementable blueprint."
  • Moving toward action. County Planning Director Thuy Shutt said the plan creates a coordination framework and "hopefully, establishes a path away from planning and recommendations to implementation."
  • Mobility advocates on board. The West Palm Beach Mobility Coalition urged the commission to receive the plan and keep collaborating with cities.

The case against

  • Plans, not solutions. "It does not give us solutions to what this board has repeatedly asked for," County Mayor Sara Baxter said. "I am not ready to adopt this master transportation plan."
  • Value for the money. "We are now on the second ask of more funding… and all we have collected so far is a framework," Commissioner Joel Flores said. Commissioner Maria Sachs said the plan mainly confirmed what's already known, that parts of the county have major traffic problems: "By God, I'm going to make sure you do your job because that's our money."
  • Rural areas left out. Some speakers from more rural areas said outreach was limited and the plan leans urban. Indian Trail Improvement District Supervisor Richard Vassalotti said it "needs to be slowed and really needs to be looked at for the sake of everyone."
  • Growth outpacing roads. At the June meeting, residents argued existing roads should be fixed before more development is approved out west.

What happens next

Commissioners asked the team to come back with detailed descriptions of projects, how much congestion each would relieve, and whether projects or funding are already in the works. Moving from a plan to implementation would also mean negotiating additional costs with WSP for management and grant writing. You can explore the plan and its project database at pbcmoves.com.
