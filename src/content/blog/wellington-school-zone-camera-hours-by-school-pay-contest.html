Got a $100 notice in the mail from Wellington, or wondering exactly when the school zone speed cameras are watching? The cameras in Wellington, Florida run all school day, not only when the yellow lights flash, and the hours are different at each school. Here's the camera schedule for every school, plus step-by-step instructions to pay or contest a notice and what happens if you ignore it.

Camera hours at every Wellington school

During arrival and dismissal, the limit is 20 mph, so the cameras flag vehicles going 31 mph or faster. During the rest of the school day, the cameras enforce the road's regular posted limit, flagging anything 11 mph or more over it.

"But the lights weren't flashing"

This is the most common complaint, and the village addresses it directly. Two things are true:

  • Cameras work all school day. Even when the school zone lights are off, the regular posted speed limit is photo-enforced while school is in session.
  • Flashing beacons aren't legally required. Under Florida law, a school zone can be enforced by the times posted on its signs. If the sign lists the hours, the 20 mph limit applies during those hours whether or not a light is flashing.

Who gets the notice, and how it's checked

  • The registered owner gets the notice in the mail, not necessarily the person who was driving.
  • A person reviews it first. The village says every violation is reviewed and approved by an authorized technician before a notice goes out.
  • Accuracy checks: the system runs daily diagnostics, and the radars are certified every year by a third party, according to the village. A malfunctioning device is pulled from enforcement until it's fixed.
  • Fines began Sept. 15, 2025, after a one-month warning period.

If you get a notice: your three options

You have 30 days from the date on the Notice of Violation.

  1. Pay the $100. Online at trafficsafetycitations.com, by phone at 1-833-965-3001 (weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.), or by mail with a check payable to the Village of Wellington. Online and phone payments carry a convenience fee. Paying closes the matter, with no points and nothing on your driving record.
  2. Request a hearing before the village's Local Hearing Officer, using the form on the village's School Zone Safety Program page. The village says the request should come from the registered owner.
  3. Submit an Affidavit of Defense (it's on the back of the notice) if someone else had the vehicle, an officer already ticketed you at the scene, or the owner had died. Include supporting documents.

Contesting pauses the 30-day payment clock while your hearing or affidavit is reviewed. You can view the photo evidence at trafficsafetycitations.com with your citation number and PIN.

Tips to avoid a notice

  • Learn the windows on your route. Middle schools start and end about 90 minutes later than elementary schools, so the 20 mph windows aren't the same from zone to zone.
  • Treat the whole school day as enforced. Between the 20 mph windows, the regular posted limit is still on camera until dismissal ends.
  • Watch the shared zone. Near Emerald Cove Middle and Equestrian Trails Elementary there are four separate 20 mph windows in one day.
  • Lending your car? The notice comes to you as the owner. If someone else was driving, the Affidavit of Defense is how you transfer responsibility, and it has to be filed within 30 days.
  • Moved recently? Notices go to the address on your vehicle registration. An old address can mean you never see the $100 notice and first hear about it as a $206 citation, so keep your registration current.
  • No school, no school-zone enforcement. The program applies on days when school is in session.
