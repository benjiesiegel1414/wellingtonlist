If you've been reporting potholes, missed trash pickups or broken street lights through GoWellington, there's a change: as of Nov. 1, 2026, the Village of Wellington has replaced it with SeeClickFix Wellington, a free app and website for non-emergency service requests. Here's what changed, how to set up the app, what you can report, what happens after you hit submit, and a few tips for getting a problem fixed faster.

What changed

Wellington worked with software company CivicPlus to set up a localized SeeClickFix portal for the village, the Town-Crier reported. Village Communications Manager David Feliciano told the paper that SeeClickFix Wellington "replaces our earlier GoWellington app with a more powerful system that tracks, manages and resolves every service request in one place."

For residents, the basic idea is the same: pick a category, mark the location, describe the problem and send it in. What's new is the tracking. Requests go straight to the right department, you get status updates by app and email as staff work on it, and you're notified when it's closed.

How it's going so far

In its first stretch after the Nov. 1 launch, residents submitted 15 requests and 11 had already been resolved, according to the Town-Crier. The most common requests were for sanitation, such as new trash bins and missed pickups, with a few maintenance reports. The village said it typically acknowledges a request in about two hours and closes it in less than a day.

"The early response has been very positive," Feliciano told the Town-Crier, adding that the village plans to add request categories and features as more residents use it.

How to set it up

  1. Download SeeClickFix for free from the Apple App Store or Google Play. You can also use it on a computer through the link on wellingtonfl.gov/seeclickfix.
  2. Search for Wellington in the app so your requests go to the village.
  3. Create an account by tapping "Profile" at the bottom right.
  4. Submit a request: choose the category that best fits, add the location, write a short description and attach a photo if you have one.
  5. Watch for updates in the app and in your email. You'll get an email when staff complete the request.

What it isn't for

  • Emergencies. Fires, crashes, crimes in progress and medical emergencies go to 911.
  • Live animals. The village doesn't remove live animals. Its Public Works FAQ points residents to the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission at (561) 625-5122, or a private trapper.
  • After-hours water emergencies. Call the village's utility numbers instead. Our who-to-call directory lists them by problem.

Code complaints can't be anonymous

The village's SeeClickFix page reminds users of a Florida law that took effect July 1, 2021 (Section 162.06, Florida Statutes): a code inspector can't open an investigation based on an anonymous complaint. If you report a possible code violation, such as an overgrown yard or an unscreened trash can, you have to give your name and address before the village investigates. For what the rules are, see our hedge, fence and code compliance guide.

The village also notes that personal information you submit may be subject to disclosure under Florida's public records law (Chapter 119), so keep descriptions factual.

Tips for a report that gets fixed fast

  • Pin the exact spot. A street address or cross street saves staff a drive around the block.
  • Add a photo. Feliciano told the Town-Crier that having the location and photos attached helps staff respond faster.
  • Check for an existing request. If someone already reported the same problem, the app tells you, and you can follow that request instead of filing a new one.
  • Pick the closest category. Requests are routed by category, so the right choice sends it to the right department the first time.
  • You can also send a compliment. The village's page invites residents to report concerns or compliments.

Is an app the best way?

Supporters of the change say an app with tracking is more transparent than a phone call: residents can see where their request stands, staff get real-time alerts, and duplicate reports are cut down, which the village says helps it plan work. Some residents, especially those who aren't comfortable with smartphones, would rather talk to a person. That option hasn't gone away. Village Hall still takes calls at (561) 791-4000, and the village says it welcomes feedback as it adds features.
