Looking for walking, running or biking trails in Wellington, Florida? Wellington has a 2.5-mile paved loop at Village Park, elevated boardwalks over wetlands at Peaceful Waters, a six-story observation tower at the Wellington Environmental Preserve and roughly 65 miles of bridle trails. Just outside the village, Okeeheelee Park adds 8 miles of bike paths and a mountain bike trail. Here's every trail worth knowing, with distances, surfaces, hours and the rules on bikes and dogs.

1. Village Park: the 2.5-mile paved loop

Village Park at 11700 Pierson Rd. has a 2.5-mile asphalt walking trail with exercise stations, which makes it Wellington's go-to for a run, a stroller walk or a workout. It's also the most convenient trail in the village: there's plenty of parking, restrooms, a playground and the new Aquatics Center next door.

  • Hours: Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.; Saturday and Sunday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
  • Good for: runners, walkers, strollers, interval workouts.

2. Peaceful Waters Sanctuary: wetland boardwalks

Inside Village Park, Peaceful Waters Sanctuary is a 30-acre wetland park with about 1,500 feet of elevated nature boardwalk and 1 mile of walking trails. Displays along the way describe South Florida's native plants and animals, and it's one of the best spots in Wellington for bird watching and nature photography. There's no admission fee.

  • Hours: follows Village Park's hours and closes daily at dusk. It's closed the second Tuesday of every month from 7 a.m. to noon for maintenance.
  • Not allowed: bicycles, rollerblades, scooters, skateboards, jogging on the boardwalk, fishing, feeding wildlife, swimming and dogs (service animals excepted).
  • Heads up: alligators live here. Feeding or bothering them is against the law.

3. Wellington Environmental Preserve: tower views and long walks

The Wellington Environmental Preserve at the Marjory Stoneman Douglas Everglades Habitat (also called Section 24), at 3491 Flying Cow Ranch Rd., is a 410-acre rainwater storage area that doubles as a nature park. A paved pedestrian path and boardwalk lead past seven Learning Areas, a large decorative trellis, a butterfly garden and a six-story observation tower with views over the marsh. There's no admission fee, and it has restrooms.

  • How it works: stormwater from 9,230 acres in the southern half of Wellington is routed here and cleaned naturally as it flows through more than two miles of wetland and marsh before heading toward the Everglades, according to the village.
  • For riders: a 3.6-mile perimeter equestrian trail connects to Wellington's bridle trail system.
  • Thanksgiving hours: 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Nov. 25-27.

Tip: it's out on the western edge of Wellington with little shade, so bring water and a hat and go early or late in the day. Read more about how the preserve fits into Wellington's drainage in our flooding and drainage explainer.

4. Pine Valley Preserve

Pine Valley Preserve at 1465 Birkdale Dr. is a small natural area with a boardwalk and a pavilion. It's an easy, quiet walk for nearby neighborhoods rather than a destination trail.

5. Town Center and Lake Wellington

The Town Center Promenade along Lake Wellington, next to the Community Center at 12150 Forest Hill Blvd., is a pleasant short walk with water views, close to Scott's Place playground, the amphitheater, the Patriot Memorial and the Veterans Memorial. The Holiday Boat Parade on Dec. 12 is best watched from here.

6. Running tracks at Olympia Park and Greenview Shores

  • Olympia Park, 9830 Stribling Way: the track is open for public use on weekdays from 6 p.m. to sunset during the school year and on weekends from 8 a.m. to sunset or park closing. In summer it's open daily from 8 a.m.
  • Greenview Shores Park, 2163 Greenview Shores Blvd., at Wellington High School: the track (not the stadium field) is open on weekends from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. during the school year, and in summer also weekdays from noon to sunset.

7. Wellington's bridle trails

Wellington is horse country, and the village maintains an equestrian trail network of about 65 miles. The village lists 15 color-coded bridle paths, from the Black, Blue and Brick Red trails to the Greenbriar, Orange, Pink, Purple, Red, White and Yellow trails, and posts a trail map on its Equestrian Trails page. These trails are built for horses, so if you walk or bike near them, give riders plenty of room. See our equestrian crossings story for more on sharing the road with horses.

8. Okeeheelee Park: the best cycling near Wellington

Okeeheelee Park at 7715 Forest Hill Blvd., just east of Wellington, is Palm Beach County's 1,660-acre regional park, and it has the most trail mileage nearby, according to the county:

  • 8-mile bicycle path and 8 miles of walking paths around the lakes
  • 4.1-mile mountain bike trail on the east side of Pinehurst Drive, south of Forest Hill Boulevard
  • A pump track and a BMX track
  • A 1-mile exercise course with 20 stations
  • A 9-mile equestrian trail
  • Okeeheelee Nature Center: 2.5 miles of trails through 90 acres of pine flatwoods and wetlands, including paved trails that work for wheelchairs and strollers and soft-surface trails into more remote areas. You don't need to pay admission to walk them.

The park is open sunrise to sunset and also has the Pooch Pines dog park.

9. Grassy Waters Preserve

For a true Everglades-style boardwalk, Grassy Waters Preserve at 8264 Northlake Blvd. in West Palm Beach has the Cypress Boardwalk Trail, a 1-mile round trip with a loop and a spur, according to the City of West Palm Beach. Hours are Monday through Saturday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and Sunday 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., and it's closed on major holidays. Restrooms are in the nature center. It's north of Wellington, off Northlake Boulevard.

Trail tips for Wellington

  • Go early. Summer heat and afternoon storms make mornings the best time to walk or ride. Winter is peak trail season.
  • Watch for wildlife. Alligators live in Wellington's canals and wetlands. Keep kids and pets back from the water's edge.
  • Know the e-bike rules. See our guide to Wellington's e-bike rules before you ride.
  • Leave the dog at home for Peaceful Waters, where dogs aren't allowed, and see our dog-friendly Wellington guide for places that welcome them.
