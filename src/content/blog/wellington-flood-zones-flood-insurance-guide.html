Is your home in a flood zone in Wellington, Florida, and do you need flood insurance? After this fall's heavy rain and standing water around the western communities, plenty of residents are asking. Here's how to look up your flood zone for free, what the zones mean, why Wellington homeowners get a discount of up to 25% on federal flood insurance, and what to do before the next storm.

Step 1: Find your flood zone

There are three free ways to check:

  1. Wellington's online flood map. The village links to an interactive map from its Current Flood Maps page; search your address to see your zone.
  2. FEMA's Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov), which has the official Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs). Palm Beach County's current maps took effect Oct. 5, 2017.
  3. A free Flood Zone Determination letter from the village. Call the Building Department at (561) 753-2430 or (561) 363-7907, or email Planning and Zoning at PZB@wellingtonfl.gov with the address and your contact information. The village can also tell you about local drainage problems, past flooding, and whether an elevation certificate is on file for the property.

The Base Flood Elevation (BFE) shown on the map is how high floodwater is expected to rise in a 100-year flood. How high your home sits compared with the BFE has a big effect on your premium in high-risk zones.

Do you need flood insurance?

If you're in a high-risk zone with a federally backed mortgage, yes. It's required under federal law. For everyone else, the village strongly recommends it. Its reasons:

  • Homeowners insurance usually doesn't cover flooding, and neither do most renters policies.
  • Low-risk doesn't mean no risk. The village notes that nearly 25% of all flood claims have historically come from outside high-risk areas, and that a home in a high-risk area has about a 2.5 times greater chance of flooding than of a fire over a 30-year mortgage.
  • It's often affordable in low- and moderate-risk zones.
  • There's usually a 30-day waiting period, unless a lender requires the policy, so you can't wait until a storm is coming.

The village says about 1,900 flood insurance policies are in force in Wellington. Ask your insurance agent about a National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policy or private flood coverage; you can also find agents in our insurance agents directory.

Why Wellington residents pay less: the CRS discount

Wellington joined FEMA's Community Rating System (CRS) in October 2003. Communities that go beyond the minimum floodplain rules earn discounts on NFIP flood insurance for their residents. Classes run from 10 (no discount) to 1 (best).

Wellington moved from Class 6 to Class 5 effective May 1, 2020, according to news coverage at the time. Palm Beach County's CRS list shows Wellington at Class 5 with a 25% discount for policies in the Special Flood Hazard Area. That puts Wellington among the best-rated communities in the county:

To keep the rating, the village has to keep up its floodplain program and report to FEMA every year.

If your zone changes

  • Low to high risk: insurance becomes mandatory for most mortgages. For policies already in place before the map change, the village says residential NFIP rate increases are typically capped at 18% a year.
  • High to low risk: insurance becomes optional, but the village still recommends it, usually at lower rates.
  • Think the map is wrong? Owners can request a Letter of Map Amendment or Revision from FEMA, typically with an elevation certificate from a surveyor.

Building or renovating in a flood zone

All development in the floodplain needs a permit under federal rules, including filling and grading. If improvements to a building cost 50% or more of its market value (a "substantial improvement"), the whole building must meet the elevation rules for new construction. New buildings in flood zones need an elevation certificate from an engineer or surveyor before they can be occupied. The village says building just 2 to 3 feet above the BFE can cut premiums by as much as half. See our building permits guide (the new HB 803 permit exemption doesn't apply to projects in flood hazard areas).

Before and during a flood

  • Sign up for Alert Wellington, the village's emergency notification system; call (561) 791-4000 to register.
  • Never drive or walk through floodwater. Six inches of moving water can knock you off your feet, and in Wellington, canals can be hard to tell apart from flooded roads.
  • Stay away from downed power lines and report them to FPL or the sheriff's office.
  • Protect your home: raise furniture and valuables, use sandbags where water collects, and keep storm drains near your home clear.
  • Drainage problems in canals and swales are handled by the Acme Improvement District. See our explainer on flooding and drainage in Wellington.

Two views on flood insurance

Many homeowners outside high-risk zones skip flood coverage to save money, reasoning that their area rarely floods and that Wellington's drainage system is designed to handle heavy rain. Floodplain managers and FEMA counter that a large share of claims come from moderate- and low-risk areas and that one flood can cost far more than years of premiums. The right answer depends on your home's elevation, location and budget, so it's worth getting a quote and looking at your own flood zone letter.
