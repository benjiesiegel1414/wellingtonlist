After a wet fall, mosquitoes have been hard to miss around Wellington, Florida. The village runs its own mosquito control program, with spray trucks, traps and larvicide, and it will give you answers by phone if you want to know when your street is being treated. Here's how Wellington decides when and where to spray, whether the spray is safe for kids and pets, how to get on the do-not-spray list, and the simple steps that cut down mosquitoes in your own yard.

How Wellington controls mosquitoes

Wellington's Environmental Services Department handles mosquito control inside the village. According to the village, its services include:

  • Ground spraying (called ULV, or ultra-low volume) from trucks to knock down adult mosquitoes
  • Ground larvicide treatments that kill mosquito larvae in standing water before they can fly
  • Barrier treatments in parks
  • Light traps and species identification to track where mosquitoes are and what kinds
  • Special event spraying

When and where trucks spray

There's no fixed spray schedule. The village says spraying is decided day by day, based on surveillance data collected each morning. State pesticide rules require that surveillance before any spraying is done.

QuestionAnswer from the village
How are mosquitoes counted?Five light traps baited with carbon dioxide run at the same time each evening. Technicians also check for larvae and take "landing rate" counts in the field.
Do resident calls matter?Yes. Residential requests help flag problems the traps don't show.
How often will my street be sprayed?No more than once a week during the June to November peak. The village is divided into five treatment zones.
How do they know what was sprayed?Each truck carries a GPS recorder showing its route and whether the sprayer was on. The record is reviewed the next day to catch missed areas.

To find your zone, use the Find Your Zone link on the village's Mosquito Control page. To report a mosquito problem or ask whether your area is scheduled, call the hotline at (561) 791-4001.

Is the spray safe for kids and pets?

The village's answer is that harm is "not likely." It says the pesticide is designed for use around warm-blooded animals. It does caution that breathing the spray could cause a reaction in people with chronic respiratory conditions such as asthma or emphysema.

The village lists the products it uses, including Altosid XR and Aquabac, which are larvicides, and Kontrol 4-4, an adulticide. Safety Data Sheets for each are available from Public Works at (561) 791-4000.

If you're concerned: a common-sense approach is to stay indoors and close windows when a truck passes, and bring pets and their water bowls inside. If you or a family member has a respiratory condition, call the hotline to ask about spraying near you.

The do-not-spray list

Beekeepers, farmers and any Wellington resident can ask to be added to the village's do-not-spray list. Submit the request through SeeClickFix Wellington, the village's service request app, which replaced the GoWellington portal on Nov. 1, 2026.

It's worth knowing both sides before you sign up. Residents who opt out often do so to protect honeybees, butterflies and other pollinators, or because of health sensitivities. On the other hand, skipping a property can mean more mosquitoes there, and for neighbors too. If you opt out, keeping up with standing water becomes even more important.

What you can do at home: drain and cover

Trucks only reach adult mosquitoes that are flying when they pass. Most mosquitoes around a home hatch within a short distance of it, often in water you didn't know was there. The Florida Department of Health's advice is to drain and cover.

Drain standing water

  • Empty anything that collects rain or sprinkler water: buckets, garbage can lids, pool covers, coolers, toys and flowerpot saucers.
  • Get rid of old tires, bottles, cans and other junk that holds water.
  • Empty and clean birdbaths and pet water bowls at least once or twice a week.
  • Keep pools chlorinated and running, and empty kiddie pools when they're not in use.
  • Cover boats and vehicles with tarps that don't trap water.

In Wellington, also check horse troughs, clogged gutters, bromeliads and low spots in the yard that stay wet after rain.

Cover up

  • Wear long sleeves, long pants, socks and shoes when mosquitoes are active, especially at dusk and dawn.
  • Use an EPA-registered repellent with DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus, para-menthane-diol or 2-undecanone, following the label.
  • For babies under 2 months, use mosquito netting instead of repellent.
  • Repair torn screens on windows, doors, porches and patios.

Dunks are sold at hardware and garden stores. For a persistent problem, a licensed pest control company can treat your yard.

Outside the village limits

Wellington's program covers the village. Unincorporated areas nearby, such as parts of The Acreage and suburban Lake Worth, are served by Palm Beach County's Mosquito Control Division, which runs its own spraying and takes service requests.
