Old paint cans in the garage, a drawer of dead batteries, a broken TV, leftover pool chemicals: none of it belongs in your Wellington garbage or recycling carts, and none of it is picked up at the curb. The good news is that Palm Beach County residents can drop off household hazardous waste and electronics for free and the nearest drop-off center is next door in Royal Palm Beach. Here's where to go, what's accepted, what isn't, and what to do with usable things you just don't want anymore.

Why these items can't go in the cart

The Village of Wellington says electronics, hazardous waste and home chemicals can leak toxic material that harms people and the environment if they're thrown away. The Solid Waste Authority (SWA), the county agency that handles disposal, adds a more immediate danger: fires. Lithium batteries that get crushed in a garbage or recycling truck can spark, and the SWA warns that a fire inside a truck or waste facility can spread quickly through the surrounding trash.

There is no curbside collection for household hazardous waste anywhere in Palm Beach County. For what does go in your carts, see our Wellington trash and recycling guide.

What you can drop off

The SWA says its centers accept most household hazardous waste, including:

  • Paints, stains, glues and solvents
  • Batteries: lithium, button, rechargeable, car and boat batteries
  • Electronics
  • Fluorescent tubes, CFL bulbs and other items containing mercury
  • Pool chemicals, pesticides and fertilizers
  • Household cleaners, rubbing alcohol and aerosol cans that aren't empty
  • Propane tanks and other compressed gas cylinders
  • Used motor oil, filters and other automotive fluids
  • Gasoline
  • Used cooking oil (handy after frying a Thanksgiving turkey)
  • Ammunition, flares and fireworks
  • Photo chemicals and boating waste such as marine batteries and expired flares

A special word on lithium batteries

Lithium batteries are in phones, laptops, power tools, e-bikes and scooters, vapes, toys, electric toothbrushes and even some greeting cards. The SWA's advice:

  • Never put them in garbage or recycling carts.
  • Tape the terminals with clear tape before you drop them off.
  • Bring them to a Home Chemical and Recycling Center. The SWA says most major retailers also accept batteries for free; look for a collection box near the entrance.
  • Very large batteries (over 300 watt-hours) may not be accepted; call first.

How to pack your car

  • Keep products in their original containers when you can, with lids on tight.
  • Don't mix chemicals.
  • Put a leaking container inside a second, sealed container.
  • Carry everything upright in the trunk or truck bed, away from passengers.

Still usable? Donate it instead

For things that work but that you no longer want, the village's Unwanted Items page has a simple message: think before you throw it away. It suggests donating appliances, clothes, electronics, furniture and toys to local organizations, or giving them away through Freecycle, a free nonprofit network that the village says has more than 2,000 registered users in the Wellington area. Old cell phones can be recycled through charity programs.

  • New toys can go to the village's holiday toy drive through Dec. 11. See our toy drive and giving guide.
  • Large items that can't be donated, such as worn-out furniture and appliances, go out with bulk pickup. See the bulk pickup rules.
  • Moving or clearing out a house? Our movers and self-storage directories can help.

Convenient enough?

Some residents would like curbside pickup or a drop-off day in Wellington itself, since a drive to Royal Palm Beach is one more errand and it's tempting to toss a battery in the trash. The SWA's position is that hazardous materials need trained handling at dedicated sites, and that keeping drop-off free and open six days a week makes it practical. The county does report strong results overall: the SWA says Palm Beach County has ranked first in Florida for recycling four years in a row.
