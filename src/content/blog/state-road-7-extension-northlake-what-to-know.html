For decades, drivers in Wellington, Royal Palm Beach and the western communities have heard that State Road 7 would one day be extended north from Okeechobee Boulevard to Northlake Boulevard. The Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) now says construction could start in 2028. The City of West Palm Beach, which gets its drinking water from the nearby Grassy Waters Preserve, has fought the project for years and is still appealing. Here's where the extension stands, what FDOT changed, and what each side has argued.

The SR 7 extension at a glance

The route
About four miles, from Okeechobee Blvd. to 60th Street North, then east along the south end of Ibis Golf & Country Club and north between Ibis and Grassy Waters Preserve to Northlake Blvd.
Phase 1
Four-laning SR 7 from Okeechobee Blvd. to 60th Street; construction planned spring 2028 to spring 2031 (about $43.4 million)
Phase 2
60th Street to Northlake Blvd.; construction planned fall 2028 to fall 2031 (about $94.2 million)
Total cost
About $137.6 million, according to FDOT
Legal status
A judge ruled for FDOT in May 2025; West Palm Beach appealed, and FDOT must reapply for a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers permit

Why it matters for Wellington

SR 7 runs along Wellington's eastern edge but stops short of Northlake Blvd. The extension would create a direct north-south link from Okeechobee Blvd. to Northlake, which supporters say would take traffic off residential roads in the western communities, the Town-Crier reported. For more on regional traffic plans, see the county transportation master plan.

What FDOT changed

At a February 2026 public meeting in Royal Palm Beach, FDOT presented a redesign aimed at environmental concerns, the Town-Crier reported. Changes include:

  • Converting more than 200 acres of right-of-way into a preservation area with a permanent conservation easement for snail kite habitat
  • Wildlife fencing and crossings along the corridor
  • About 709 acres of wetland restoration to offset 56.7 acres of wetland impact
  • No direct stormwater outfalls into the Ibis lakes or Grassy Waters, plus a retaining wall
  • A narrower median, noise abatement measures and a shared-use path north of 60th Street
  • A redesigned Ibis Spillway Bridge with no pilings in the water, and a roundabout at the east entrance to Ibis

The case for the extension

  • A redesigned project. "Permitting has been a significant challenge," FDOT project manager Scott Peterson said. "[But] we have significantly redesigned the project to address the concerns of the community."
  • The court sided with FDOT. In a May 2025 ruling after a seven-week trial, Administrative Law Judge Francine Ffolkes found that West Palm Beach "failed to meet its ultimate burden of persuasion" on flooding, water quality, wetlands and wildlife, WLRN reported.
  • Western traffic relief. Supporters, including Palm Beach County, say the western communities have serious congestion and need another north-south route to keep traffic off residential streets.
  • Opinion is shifting. "To go from highly negative 10 years ago to concerned but interested is a move in the right direction," Peterson told WPTV.

The case against

  • Drinking water. West Palm Beach has long argued that building the road next to Grassy Waters Preserve, which stores the city's drinking water before treatment, could risk flooding or pollution, the Town-Crier and WLRN have reported.
  • Wildlife and wetlands. The city has presented expert testimony disputing that FDOT's plans would protect the preserve, according to Stet News.
  • Neighbors. Some of the strongest opposition has come from Ibis residents, who live along the planned route.
  • Unfinished legal process. The city appealed the 2025 ruling to the Fourth District Court of Appeal, and FDOT must still get a new Army Corps permit after the previous one was revoked in 2020. Peterson acknowledged FDOT can't rule out more court delays.

What happens next

FDOT plans to finish the design of both phases in August 2027, with construction starting in 2028 if permits and legal challenges allow. You can follow the project at sr7extension.com.
