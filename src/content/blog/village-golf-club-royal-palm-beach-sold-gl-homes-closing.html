The Village Golf Club in Royal Palm Beach, a public course just north of Wellington, has been sold to homebuilder GL Homes for $14.5 million and plans to stop golf operations on Sunday, Dec. 6, 2026. Royal Palm Beach officials say no redevelopment has been approved, but they're worried Florida's new Infill Redevelopment Act could let a housing project move forward without public hearings. Here's what we know about the sale, what the law allows, what both sides are saying, and what golfers and neighbors can do now.

Village Golf Club, 122 Country Club Dr., Royal Palm Beach; public 18 holes, about 175 acres

GL Homes, $14.5 million, closed Nov. 3, 2026, according to the South Florida Business Journal and Traded via Best Clubhouse
Last day of golf
Anticipated Sunday, Dec. 6, 2026 (subject to change), per the club's website

Redevelopment plan

The law in play
Florida's Infill Redevelopment Act (SB 1434)

What happened

After 35 years in Royal Palm Beach, the Village Golf Club announced on its website that it "will be winding down its golf operations," with an anticipated final day of Sunday, Dec. 6, 2026. The club thanked golfers "for 35 wonderful years" and said the date is subject to change.

The news came days after the property changed hands. GL Homes bought the roughly 175-acre course for $14.5 million, the Town-Crier reported on Nov. 13. Best Clubhouse, citing the commercial real estate database Traded and the South Florida Business Journal, reported that the deal closed on Nov. 3. GL Homes has not publicly released a plan, a home count or a site plan for the property, and we did not find a public statement from the company about its intentions.

The Village of Royal Palm Beach was not part of the private sale and had not received any redevelopment proposal as of the Town-Crier's report.

How we got here

  • 2018: Developer 13th Floor Homes proposed 450 homes on the southern part of the course. The plan drew heavy opposition from residents and never moved forward, according to the Town-Crier.
  • May 21, 2026: Gov. Ron DeSantis signed the Infill Redevelopment Act (SB 1434), which applies only in Palm Beach, Broward and Miami-Dade counties.
  • October 2026: Royal Palm Beach staff met with 13th Floor Homes about a possible redevelopment under the new law. At the Oct. 15 Village Council meeting, Village Manager Ray Liggins said he could spend up to $50,000 to hire a law firm to review any application.
  • Nov. 3, 2026: GL Homes closes on the $14.5 million purchase.
  • November 2026: The club notifies members and posts that golf operations will wind down, with Dec. 6 as the anticipated final day.

What the Infill Redevelopment Act allows

The new state law lets certain properties, including closed golf courses surrounded by homes, be redeveloped for housing through an administrative approval process instead of the usual public hearings and council votes. Key points, based on a Holland and Knight summary and Town-Crier reporting:

  • The one-year rule: a golf course must be out of operation or use for at least a year before it can qualify. The Village Golf Club is still open, so it would not qualify today. How or whether the new owner could meet the requirement is unclear, the Town-Crier reported.
  • Density: the Town-Crier reported the law could allow up to 10 units per acre at this site, or up to about 1,750 homes, depending on how the law is interpreted. That would make it one of the largest residential communities in Royal Palm Beach. Holland and Knight's summary says density is capped at the lower of the average density of neighboring residential zoning or 25 units per acre, so the actual number would depend on the surrounding neighborhoods.
  • Protections for neighbors: projects next to single-family homes need a buffer of at least 20 feet kept as open space, adjacent owners get 90 days to buy a closed recreation site and keep it as open space, and developers pay double the parks impact fee.

Why Royal Palm Beach and neighbors are worried

Royal Palm Beach leaders stress that a sale isn't an approval. "The sale raises understandable questions about the future of the property, but the sale itself does not mean that a redevelopment plan has been approved," Mayor Jeff Hmara told the Town-Crier. "Our concern is that the Infill Redevelopment Act could allow a qualifying project to proceed through an administrative process without the public hearings, public input or council decisions that residents expect for other developments."

Councilwoman Selena Samios called for "an independent legal review of the Florida Infill Redevelopment Act, the property's eligibility and whether all applicable requirements are being met."

Neighbors say they bought homes around a golf course and want a say in what replaces it. One resident who lives near the course told the Town-Crier he expects the village "to fight this sale and closure." Residents are organizing a two-part campaign to support a legal challenge by the village and raise public awareness, the paper reported.

Earlier, Hmara also raised concerns about traffic, especially on Okeechobee Boulevard, if large numbers of homes were added.

The case for redevelopment

Supporters of the law see it differently. The Legislature found that South Florida's urban areas lack enough land for new housing, and that faster approvals on already-developed or underused parcels serve the public interest. Building on a former golf course uses existing roads, water and sewer lines instead of pushing development onto undeveloped land farther west. Supporters also point to the law's built-in protections, including buffers, the neighbors' option to buy the land and double parks fees. And a property owner generally has the right to sell, and the buyer has the right to apply under state law; whether any specific project qualifies is a question the village and, possibly, the courts will decide.

It's also worth noting what hasn't happened: no application has been filed, and GL Homes hasn't announced what, if anything, it plans to build.

What golfers need to know

  • Play while you can: the club says tee times continue until operations end, with Dec. 6 as the anticipated last day.
  • Use prepaid benefits: the club says Gold Card complimentary rounds, range cards, gift cards and other prepaid golf benefits will be honored during the remaining operations, subject to their existing terms. Updates will be posted on its website.
  • Other public golf nearby: Okeeheelee Park on Forest Hill Boulevard has a 27-hole county golf course (three nine-hole courses) with a driving range, chipping area, putting green and pro shop, according to Palm Beach County Parks.

Why this matters in Wellington

The Village Golf Club is a short drive from Wellington, and many Wellington residents have played there for years. It's also the second western-communities golf course in the spotlight under the new law. In Wellington, BH Group is looking to use the same law to turn the shuttered Polo West Golf Course into a luxury residential community. More homes north of Southern Boulevard could also mean more traffic on routes Wellington drivers use, like Okeechobee Boulevard and State Road 7. What to watch next

  • Whether the course actually closes Dec. 6, and what happens to the land afterward.
  • Whether GL Homes files a redevelopment application, and whether Royal Palm Beach's outside legal review finds the property eligible.
  • Whether the village or residents pursue a legal challenge to the law.
  • Royal Palm Beach Village Council meetings, where residents can speak during public comment.
