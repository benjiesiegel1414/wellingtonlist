How does trash pickup in Wellington, Florida work? Garbage is collected twice a week, and recycling, yard waste and bulk trash once a week each, through the village's contracted hauler. The rules on timing, weight and what goes where are strict, and piles that break them get tagged instead of picked up. Here's the complete guide: collection schedule, what goes in each bin, bulk item rules, yard waste limits, hazardous waste, holiday changes and how to report a missed pickup.

Find your collection days

Wellington is divided into collection zones. Use the village's Garbage Collection Zone Lookup map on its Solid Waste and Recycling page: enter your address and click your property to see your garbage, recycling, yard waste and bulk days. The current zones took effect Dec. 30, 2021, when FCC Environmental Services became Wellington's solid waste hauler.

When to put carts out (and bring them back)

  • Out: no earlier than 6 p.m. the day before collection. The village's Garbage page says carts must be out by 6 a.m. on collection day (its printed guidelines say 7 a.m.), so the safest move is to set them out the night before.
  • In: carts must be off the curb and stored by 11 p.m. on collection day, and carts kept outside must be screened from view.
  • Bulk and extra yard waste: no more than 24 hours before your collection day.

Garbage: the gray cart

  • Only garbage inside the gray automated cart is collected on garbage days. Bags or items outside the cart wait for bulk day.
  • Face the cart correctly: wheels and handle toward the house, lid arrows toward the street.
  • Place it within 3 feet of the curb and at least 4 feet from mailboxes, other carts and recycling bins.
  • Max weight: 50 pounds. Nothing on top of the lid, and nothing longer than 6 feet.
  • Bag light trash like paper scraps, cups and foam so it doesn't blow away.
  • Coconuts, pine needles and grass go in the garbage cart, not yard waste.
  • Never put in liquids, paint, certain batteries, propane tanks, explosives or corrosives. You can be held responsible for damage or injuries.
  • The cart belongs to the village. Don't paint or modify it, and leave it behind if you move.

Big boxes that aren't cut and flattened go out on bulk day. Single-family homes get small curbside bins; large recycling containers are only for multifamily complexes. Florida's Solid Waste Authority of Palm Beach County has a more detailed list of recyclables.

Yard waste (vegetation)

Yard waste is debris from routine yard maintenance only, picked up once a week.

  • Containerize everything small: leaves, pine needles, hedge trimmings, small fronds and weeds go in bags or your green vegetation cart. In areas with automated vegetation pickup, fill the green cart first, then pile the rest.
  • Size limits: no piece over 6 feet long or 50 pounds, and no limbs over 10 inches in diameter.
  • Keep piles separate: 3 feet from other trash, not under power lines and not on vacant lots, canal banks or main roads.
  • Not yard waste: tree removal, trunks, stumps, root balls, land clearing, sod, dirt, rocks, pallets and concrete edging. Landscapers and tree contractors must haul away their own debris.

Tagged piles: piles that break the rules get tagged. You can fix the pile for the next pickup or pay the hauler $9 per cubic yard to take it as is. Dumping debris on someone else's property, vacant land or canal banks can be treated as illegal dumping under Florida's litter law.

Bulk trash

Bulk is for items too big for your cart, once a week. Put it out no more than 24 hours ahead, in front of your home.

  • Accepted: refrigerators, ranges, washers and dryers, water heaters, sofas, mattresses, toilets, carpet (cut into 4-foot lengths, under 50 pounds per roll), wood under 6 feet with nails removed, and damaged village carts (empty and upside down).
  • Home repair debris: up to 4 cubic yards, about a small pickup bed, neatly stacked, cut under 6 feet and at least 3 feet from mailboxes and carts. Bag loose drywall and tile in containers under 50 pounds each.
  • Not collected: concrete and rock.
  • Safety: remove or tape shut appliance doors, and break glass and mirrors into a bag, blanket or tarp inside your cart.

Bigger cleanouts or renovations need a roll-off dumpster from a hauler permitted by the village; the village lists permitted haulers in its business directory.

Electronics and hazardous waste

Paint, pool chemicals, household cleaners, propane tanks, fluorescent bulbs, used motor oil and electronics don't go in any Wellington cart. Take them to a Solid Waste Authority of Palm Beach County drop-off site; check locations on its website or call (561) 697-2700.

Holiday schedule

There's no garbage, recycling or vegetation pickup on Thanksgiving Day or Christmas Day. If your day falls on one of them, service resumes on your next regular collection day; there's no makeup day. For Thanksgiving 2026 details, see our Thanksgiving in Wellington guide.

Hurricane debris

  • Before the season: finish major tree trimming by April 30, and thin foliage so wind can pass through.
  • Once a storm is named: don't cut trees, start projects or clean out the garage, because regular pickup may stop and trucks can't run in sustained winds over 35 mph. Secure or bring in your carts.
  • After the storm: keep garbage, recycling, vegetation and construction debris in separate piles, away from power lines, water meters, mailboxes and hydrants, and never in the road. Solid Waste Authority crews remove storm debris, which can take weeks or months.
  • Fallen trees on your property are the homeowner's or HOA's responsibility.

Report a missed pickup

Report missed garbage, recycling, yard waste or bulk pickups through SeeClickFix, the village's online service request system, or call Wellington Solid Waste at (561) 791-4003 with questions about what's allowed.
